# LKO · Lucknow, Uttar Pradesh: city bible

Research for Level Devil Dad, chapter 17. Facts carry a link or say (unverified). Checked against
`docs/MECHANICS.md` and the Done tables. Hooks kept for Lucknow in this batch: politeness (pehle aap), the old
maze as a generic shape, chikankari's washable print, makhan malai, Kathak footwork, the Taj (as a generic white
dome, state-wide). Bangles (JAI-05, HYD-07), brass (RNC-06), paan (`paan`), kites and biryani are taken: leave
Firozabad, Moradabad and Banaras paan alone.

## 1. Identity
- To Lakhnawis: the city of tehzeeb (manners), where even an argument starts with "aap"; Nawabi gates, slow
  evenings in Hazratganj, chikan kurtas and kebabs that melt.
- Visitors come for: the Bara Imambara's maze, Rumi Darwaza, Chowk's food, Aminabad shopping, and the state
  beyond: Agra's Taj, Fatehpur Sikri, Dudhwa's swamp deer.
- Proud and laughing at: "pehle aap, pehle aap" until the train leaves; "Muskuraiye, aap Lucknow mein hain"
  (smile, you're in Lucknow; a city welcome line, unverified source).

## 3. Everyday life hooks
- **Transport.** Tempos and e-rickshaws in the old city, metro on the north-south line (unverified), tongas
  long gone from most roads (unverified). Trains are taken: no Charbagh station gags.
- **Street food.** Galouti kebab (so soft it melts), makhan malai/nimish (milk foam set outdoors on winter
  nights, served at dawn, gone by mid-morning when it warms) (unverified details from food writers), basket
  chaat, sheermal, kulfi with falooda.
  - Hidden-item candidates: **makhan malai** (first choice), **sheermal**, **galouti**.
  - Traps: makhan malai: a floor of foam that holds only in the cold morning and sinks as the sun climbs (the
    stage's light gets warmer and you read it); galouti: platforms that collapse when pressed for long (melts
    in the mouth = melts under the foot; but DMN-03 melting ice is taken, so make it a *pressure* rule: a soft
    tile goes flat after one second of weight and springs back when you leave? no: springs are taken. Keep it
    to "goes flat and stays flat"); kulfi: matka pot sold in a stack.
- **Markets.** Chowk and Aminabad, cloth bolts, chikan stalls, attar bottles (keep perfume as a smell-cloud? no
  wind. Skip).
- **Weather.** Cold foggy winter mornings; loo summers; evenings in Hazratganj.
- **Animals.** Rhesus monkeys (taken), pigeons over the old city (pigeon flying is a Lucknow hobby, kabootarbazi,
  unverified), barasingha in Dudhwa.
- **Crafts.** Chikankari: a pattern is block-printed on muslin, embroidered, then the piece is washed to remove
  every trace of the print; shadow work (bakhiya) is stitched on the back and shows as a shadow on the front;
  32 stitches incl. jali where threads are pulled apart without piercing ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chikan_(embroidery))).
  Trap: the blue printed lines are platforms *until the wash*. BPL-04's needle is the player; here you never sew.
- **Sports and games.** Pigeon flying from rooftops (unverified), kabaddi (taken), chess-like pachisi (Fatehpur
  court, unverified).
- **Festivals.** Lucknow Mahotsav; Kathak (Lucknow gharana: graceful, expressive, lots of chakkar spins and
  footwork with ghungroo, unverified detail). No religious festivals as traps (Muharram, Diwali, Eid: avoid).
- **Habits and sayings.** "Pehle aap" (after you); "aap" to everyone, even children; "janab"; "tashreef rakhiye"
  (please be seated); a polite argument that never ends.

## 5. Twelve stage concepts
**1. Aadaab Lucknow (Greetings, Lucknow)** · tour · Rumi Darwaza, Hazratganj, the Gomti. Betrayal: a stroller
ahead stops and bows to you; if you don't stop (stand still for a beat) he keeps bowing and you can't pass, and
the stroller behind walks into you. Second try: stop, let him finish, go. Closest: none in the stop/look family
(those freeze when *you* look or don't move); here the rule is a social turn.

**2. Pehle Aap (After you)** · standard · A doorway and two gentlemen. Expect: walk through. Betrayal: each
NPC at a doorway waits for the other; when you arrive, both bow to you and step *into* the doorway together,
blocking it, until you say "pehle aap" by bowing (standing still). Then they go first and the door shuts behind
them on a timer. FIX: the dad who waits too long gets "pehle aap" said *to* him and is pushed through first
into the spikes beyond. Act: a corridor of six doorways, each politer. Door: `pehle`: the door opens only after
someone else goes through it. Closest: CHN-02 queue; different: you control the order with politeness, not
place-saving.

**3. Bhool Bhulaiya (The old maze)** · "how would I know" · Generic three-level arches. Expect: pick the right
arch (taken as `fakewin`). Betrayal: every arch is right; what matters is the *height* you enter at: enter an arch
by jumping and you come out on the upper level, walking and you stay. Second try: walk in. Second betrayal: one
arch has a lower ceiling past it: jump in there and you hit the ceiling. Door: the 489th doorway, the only one
you reach from above. Closest: `invisible`, `backdoor`; different: no fake door; the way you enter routes you.

**4. Chhapa (The block print)** · standard · Chikan workshop. Expect: blue lines are decoration. Betrayal: the
printed pattern is a floor of blue lines; the stitched (white) parts are solid; at the washerman's whistle
the print is washed off and only what was stitched remains. Second try: cross on white, not blue. FIX: the dad
who waits for stitches gets the second wash, which also takes the jali (open-work) tiles that are only pulled
threads. Act: a stitcher fills in the pattern ahead of you, a stitch at a time; follow just behind her.
Door: `bakhiya`: the door is stitched on the back of the cloth; you see only its shadow on the front and walk
into the shadow. Closest: BPL-04 (the needle is you), DMN-07 (path fades behind the brush); different: the
pattern's state (printed/stitched/washed) is the rule and you never sew.

**5. Makhan Malai (Winter foam)** · standard · Dawn in Chowk. Expect: a slow, soft stage. Betrayal: foam
tiles hold only while the light is cold blue; as dawn warms, they sink one by one from the sunny side. Second
try: cross from the shady side first. Second betrayal: the vendor's brass tray lid lifts and lets the sun on
a row you thought was in shade. Door: the foam cup, which you reach before the sun does. Closest: DMN-03
melting ice; different: driven by light direction and shadow lines from buildings, not time on the tile.

**6. Kathak (The spin and the bells)** · long · A stage show. Expect: a beat to hop to (JAI-02 is taken).
Betrayal: the floor tiles are ghungroo (ankle bells) strips; landing on a ringing strip makes the dancer
spin (chakkar) toward the sound; her spin sweeps the stage. Second try: land on silent strips (no ring
lines). Act: tatkar: she taps a rhythm and the strips she taps ring next; you go where she isn't tapping.
Door: she ends on a pose (sam); the door opens on her last stamp, under her heel. Closest: JAI-02, KOH note;
different: the sound you make draws the dancer; no forced hops.

**7. Hazratganj (Evening stroll)** · marathon · Long colonnade. Expect: run fast. Betrayal: the street has a
"ganjing" pace: run and the strollers close ranks to tut at you, walk and they part. Second try: walk.
Second betrayal: the lamplighter's ladder blocks the arcade at each lamp you pass at walking pace. FIX: the dad
who walks everywhere is under the falling awning at dusk. Door: the café door, held for you (you must say "pehle
aap" to the doorman). Closest: musical-chairs freeze, BOM-03 crowd; different: your speed sets the crowd's
shape.

**8. Galouti (So soft it melts)** · "how would I know" · Kebab stall. Expect: hot griddle trap. Betrayal: the
kebab platforms flatten under you after a second and stay flat; flattened kebabs make a floor *lower* than the
spikes' tips beside them. Second try: flatten a row first and walk the flat row. Door: the sheermal flat
bread: you walk across its crust. Closest: crumbling floors; different: it lowers, stays, and becomes a safe
lane you build.

**9. Taj (The white dome)** · "how would I know" · Agra, generic dome and pool. Expect: run to the dome.
Betrayal: the dome shrinks as you come closer, and its ledges with it; from far away its terrace is a platform
you can't reach; up close it's too small to land on. Second try: jump for the terrace from the exact spot
where it's your size (marked by a paving line). Door: the gate behind you: you turn round, and from the dome
the gate is huge. Closest: `tiny`; different: Dad stays the same; distance scales the world.

**10. Kabootarbazi (Rooftop pigeons)** · long · Old-city roofs. Expect: dodge birds (crows are chasers already).
Betrayal: the flock follows the flyer's flag: red flag circle, white flag land; they land on the roof you
were jumping to. Second try: watch the flag. Second betrayal: the flag is two-sided and the wind turns it
(no wind as a force: only the flag's colour turns). Door: the pigeon loft door, shut while birds are home.
Unverified hobby details: check with locals.

**11. Pachisi Court (Fatehpur Sikri)** · standard · Life-size board-game courtyard (unverified). Expect: free
grid. Betrayal: cowrie shells are thrown and you may move only as many squares as fall mouth-up; other pieces
are courtiers who knock you back if you share a square. Second try: wait for a bigger throw. Door: the home
square in the centre. Closest: PNJ-06 roulette floors; different: count-based moves, pieces that capture.

**12. Lakhnavi Tehzeeb (Lucknow manners, finale)** · finale · Callbacks only to LKO: the pehle aap doorways,
the chikan print that washes, the malai that sinks with the sun, the Bhool Bhulaiya arches by height, the
Kathak bells. New twist: the politeness and the bells clash: bowing (standing still) on a ringing strip makes
the dancer spin to you.

Picks: 1 + 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 12.

## 6. Look and sound
- Palette: sky `#E9D8C4` (winter haze), hills/far buildings `#C9A27E` (sandstone and lime plaster), tiles
  `#F3EEE6` (chikan white), accent `#5A8F7B` (Nawabi green), second accent `#C2485B` (rose of the kulfi).
- Skyline: tall gateway with a flower crest, domes, arches, colonnade, a thin clock tower in the distance,
  a far white dome for Agra stages. Ground cap: cream lime-plaster kerb with a carved border.
- Weather: dense winter fog mornings; golden evenings.
- Local script name: लखनऊ (Devanagari) / لکھنؤ (Urdu).
- Music: 84 bpm, Hindustani light-classical, raga Yaman family (unverified fit), tabla on teentaal (16 beats),
  sarangi lead, ghungroo on the sam.
- Postcard line: "Pehle aap, Dad. No, really. Pehle aap."

## 7. Avoid (hard no)
Religion (the Imambara is a religious site: draw only a generic maze; no Muharram, no shrines, no Ayodhya,
Varanasi or Mathura); communal topics; politics; real shop names (kebab and chaat houses); real people (poets,
nawabs by name); film references; the Residency and 1857 as gags; "UP" stereotypes about crime or
roughness; bangles, brass and paan (other cities have them).
