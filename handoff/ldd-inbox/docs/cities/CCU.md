# CCU · Kolkata, West Bengal: city bible

Research for Level Devil Dad, chapter 19. Facts carry a link or say (unverified). Checked against
`docs/MECHANICS.md` and the Done tables. Taken and left alone: overhead wire (`bom03-wire`), trains and metro
(BOM, BLR-03, CHN-08), Hooghly ferries (boats everywhere), waterlogging (BOM-04, CHN-03), football (PNJ-03,
`penalty`), falling things (rockfall kit), festival lights (BLR-06), tigers that stalk your back (GNR-05: so no
Sundarbans back-of-head masks), Darjeeling's toy train (trains). The pontoon bridge went to PAT; the "vehicle
that never stops, you hop off" rule is Kolkata's (the bus conductor's "aaste"). Hooks kept for CCU: the tram's
pole and rails, Howrah Bridge's porters, College Street's book stacks, Kumartuli's straw-and-clay frames, adda,
phuchka, mishti doi in a clay bhanr, the pandal's bamboo skeleton.

## 1. Identity
- To Kolkatans: the City of Joy, of adda over tea, books by the kilo on College Street, trams that still crawl,
  and five days of pandal-hopping every autumn.
- Visitors come for: Howrah Bridge, Victoria Memorial, the Maidan, Kumartuli, College Street, Park Street, and
  the state beyond: Darjeeling's hills, the Sundarbans, Shantiniketan, Bishnupur's terracotta.
- Proud and laughing at: arguing for three hours over one cup of tea; "aaste!" as the bus speeds up; every
  Kolkatan's opinion on which phuchka is the best (no names).

## 3. Everyday life hooks
- **Transport.** Private buses with a conductor at the door who shouts the stops and slaps the side; "aaste"
  (slowly) tells the driver to slow so people can step off while it rolls (unverified that it never stops;
  common lore). Yellow taxis (old boxy saloon shape, unbranded). Hand-pulled rickshaws: **never a gag**. Trams.
- **Street food.** Phuchka (Kolkata's pani puri: tamarind water, mashed potato), jhalmuri (puffed rice mix in a
  paper cone), kathi roll, telebhaja (fritters), mishti doi (sweet curd set in a clay bhanr), sandesh (pressed
  in carved moulds) (unverified details).
  - Hidden-item candidates: **mishti doi** (first choice), **jhalmuri**, **kathi roll**.
  - Traps: phuchka: the vendor hands one each time you stop in front of him, and you must eat it (a pause)
    before you can move; jhalmuri: the seller shakes the tin and the puffed rice jumps (a floor of light grains
    that rise when shaken); mishti doi: bhanr pots are stacked and the top one is always being taken.
- **Markets.** New Market crowds (unverified), Gariahat pavement stalls.
- **Weather.** Sticky monsoon, waterlogging (taken), cold winter mornings with fog over the Maidan.
- **Animals.** Crows (chasers are taken), street dogs (taken), the zoo (skip).
- **Crafts.** Kumartuli clay, Baluchari weaving, kantha embroidery (running stitch on old saris, unverified),
  Bankura terracotta horse (unverified).
- **Sports.** Para football (taken), cricket (taken), carrom on the pavement (unverified that it's Kolkata-specific;
  common in Bengal adda corners).
- **Games.** Kumir-danga (crocodile and land: a tag game where the "crocodile" can't come on the "land" bits;
  unverified local rules); lattu (taken).
- **Festivals.** Durga Puja (pandals, crowds, dhak drums, no deities, no immersion gags), Poila Baishakh (new
  year, unverified), Christmas on Park Street (lights taken).
- **Adda.** Endless chat on a rok (the raised platform outside old houses, unverified term), tea in clay cups.
- **Sayings.** "Ki khobor?", "Darun!", "Aaste!", "Dada", "Ektu", "Chol", "Ki holo?".

## 5. Twelve stage concepts
**1. Nomoshkar Kolkata** · tour · Howrah Bridge, a tram, the Maidan, Victoria's dome. Betrayal: the tram rails
are grooves; a foot that lands in a groove sticks for a beat, and the tram is behind you. Second try: land
between rails. Closest: mud you can't jump from (GNR-06); different: it's only the rail lines, and it's a
one-beat catch, timed with the tram.

**2. Aaste! (Slowly!)** · standard · Private bus. Expect: it stops at the stop. Betrayal: it never stops;
"aaste" slows it to a crawl for a moment; you must step off then, and step off *facing forward* (walk in the
bus's direction) or you fall. Second try: walk off forward on "aaste". FIX: the dad who hops off forward meets
the pole at the next stop's kerb. Act: three buses overtaking each other, each conductor shouting a different
stop. Door: the stop is never where the sign is: it's where the conductor shouts. Closest: TVM-02 bells, HYD
autos; different: you exit a moving thing, and the shout is the timing.

**3. Trolley Pole** · long · On a tram's roof and in front of it. Expect: a slow, safe ride. Betrayal: at every
junction the pole jumps the wire; the tram stops dead and the pole swings round, sweeping the roof; the
conductor walks back with the rope to put it on again. Second try: drop to the step at junctions. Second
betrayal: the rails switch at the points and the tram takes the other line. Door: Esplanade loop: the tram
turns, the door is on the inside of the turn. Closest: `bom03-wire` (wire), CHN-08 train roof; different: the
wire isn't the hazard, the swinging pole is, and it's predictable at junctions.

**4. Boi Para (Book street)** · standard · College Street stacks. Expect: book avalanche (taken as rockfall).
Betrayal: nothing falls; customers ask for a title and the seller pulls it from the bottom of a stack: the
stack drops one book's height and whatever stands on it drops too, into the spikes between stalls? No: into
the next customer's view; the drop is a ledge that vanishes. Second try: stand on stacks no one is browsing.
FIX: the dad who waits on a quiet stack gets the bookseller's "second hand" stack re-shelved on top of him.
Act: a stall where books are moved stack to stack as a seller sorts them. Door: the book you were looking for:
the door is a gap where it was pulled. Closest: crumbling and dropping floors; different: the customer chooses
the stack, one book at a time.

**5. Kumartuli (The potters' lane)** · long · Straw skeletons, clay layers. Expect: statues as platforms.
Betrayal: straw is air; clay is solid; the potter adds a layer where he's working, so a figure gets more
solid in front of you, never behind. Second betrayal: the sun dries a layer and it cracks off (a dry layer
breaks when you land from height). Second try: walk on wet grey clay, not on dry pale clay. Door: the finished
figure is wheeled out of the workshop and the door is behind it. Note: generic figures only (horses, people,
no deities). Closest: RNC-06 lost-wax (hollow window); different: build-up layers, wet vs dry.

**6. Phuchka (The vendor)** · standard · Phuchka stand. Expect: free food. Betrayal: stop in front of the vendor
and he hands you one: you must eat (a one-second pause) and you're stuck while the crowd behind pushes you.
Second try: never stop in front of him. Second betrayal: the crowd forms round him in a ring you can only cross
by stopping (and eating). Door: the leaf bowl: the door is behind a stack of them, and taking one opens it.
Closest: AMR-01 being fed; different: a forced pause, not a shorter jump.

**7. Howrah Bridge (The porters)** · marathon · The bridge's footpath. Expect: dodge pedestrians. Betrayal:
porters with head-loads stream both ways; the loads are platforms moving at walking pace, and only the top of
a load is safe (the road below has cars). Second try: ride the loads going your way. Second betrayal: a porter
sets his load down to rest when you stand on it for long. FIX: the dad who switches early meets the porter going
the other way. Door: at the Howrah end the stream turns into the station: you get off before. Closest: DMN-04
plank hand to hand, BOM-02; different: two streams, rest stops, you choose by direction.

**8. Adda (The endless chat)** · "how would I know" · A rok outside an old house. Expect: chatting old men are
scenery. Betrayal: sit next to them (stand still on the rok) and you join the adda: you can't leave until the
topic changes, and the topic changes only when a tea seller passes. Second try: never stop on the rok. Second
betrayal: the rok is the only safe route over the flooded lane (not water: a dug-up road). Door: the tea
seller's clay cup: the door opens when you buy one. Closest: RPR-03 (time only while you move); different: a
social trap that holds you, released by an outside event.

**9. Pandal (The bamboo skeleton)** · marathon · A pandal under construction. Expect: climb the scaffold.
Betrayal: workers tie cloth panels on to the bamboo in the order they need: the panel goes up exactly where your
next foothold was, a wall you can't pass. Second try: climb where the cloth is already tied (it's a solid wall
you can stand on top of). Second betrayal: the night of opening, the queue. Door: the gate opens on the dhak
beat. Respect: no deities, no puja, only the structure and the crowd.

**10. Bhanr (The clay cup)** · standard · Mishti doi stall. Expect: stacked pots. Betrayal: the top pot is
always being taken by a customer; you stand on the second from top and the stack grows as he restocks. Second
try: stand on the second pot. Door: you leave the cup on the counter; the door opens. Closest: JAI-03 pots on
head; different: you stand on the stack and the stack is shop stock.

**11. Kumir-danga (Crocodile and land)** · "how would I know" · Children's game (unverified rules). Expect: the
"land" squares are safe. Betrayal: the "crocodile" (a child) can't step on land, but the land squares are
chalked and rain washes chalk off; squares the crocodile sees you on become water. Second try: hop between
squares when he looks away? No: the look rule is taken. Use: when you call "land" (stand still) you're safe for
three seconds only. Check rules with natives before building.

**12. Kolkata Kolkata (Finale)** · finale · Callbacks only to CCU: the never-stopping bus and "aaste", the
tram pole at junctions, the porters' loads on Howrah Bridge, the book stacks that drop, Kumartuli clay, the
adda that holds you. New twist: the porters' loads are book bundles from College Street, and customers ask for
them mid-crossing.

Picks: 1 + 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 12.

## 6. Look and sound
- Palette: sky `#C9D6DF` (humid grey-blue), hills/far city `#8D99AE` (smog), tiles `#B5651D` (old red brick), accent
  `#F2C14E` (taxi yellow), second accent `#1D5C96` (tram blue-and-white), clay `#8C7A6B`.
- Skyline: a giant cantilever bridge, crumbling colonial buildings with green shutters, a white dome with a
  figure on top, a tram wire across the frame, bamboo pandal skeletons.
- Ground cap: tram rails in cobbled road; cracked pavement with clay cups.
- Weather: monsoon drizzle, winter fog over the Maidan, sticky haze.
- Local script name: কলকাতা (Bengali).
- Music: 110 bpm, Baul-flavoured folk, major pentatonic (unverified fit), dhak (the big puja drum) and khol,
  ektara drone, a tram bell as the downbeat sound effect.
- Postcard line: "The tram is slower than you, and still gets there first."

refugees; Odisha-Bengal rasagola rivalry; real sweet shops and phuchka stalls; real writers and film directors.
