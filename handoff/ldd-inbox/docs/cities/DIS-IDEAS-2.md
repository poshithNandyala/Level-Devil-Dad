### 1. Dheki (The husking lever) — slot 07, the MOMENTUM beat; 05 alternate
- **Place:** an Uruka courtyard the night before Bhogali Bihu; women pound rice for pitha flour on a row of three dhekis (the dheki is the Assamese foot-operated husking lever; it is used to make rice powder for pithas: [Wikipedia: Dheki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dheki)).
- **Rule:** each dheki is a 1.8 m wooden lever on a pedestal; the fulcrum sits five-eighths of the way from the pestle (same source). A woman presses the tail: the pestle rises; she steps off: it falls by its own weight into the mortar. One woman gives a slow beat, "two or three women" (same source) a fast one, and the second woman joins a few seconds in.
- **Decision:** On a machine driven by someone else's foot, the only still tile is the fulcrum; cross each dheki by its pedestal, in the beat between falls.
- **Obvious play:** hop across the mortar while the pestle is up: it falls by its weight, no wind-up, so the hop lands under it. Death cause `dis-dheki`.
- **Obvious fix:** ride the tail end (it looks like a plank): when the woman steps off, the tail flies up and throws Dad into the next dheki's pestle (a different trap, different tick: `dis-dheki-tail`). A second fix, keep the first tempo, fails when the second woman joins (the beat quickens: the 07 dhol already owns "the beat quickens", so the builder should make the join change the *pattern* (pestle 2 falls off-beat), not the speed).
- **One-move answer:** stand on the pedestal (fulcrum) of each dheki, wait one fall, step to the next pedestal while the pestle is down and the tail is up.
- **Closest:** GNR-10 `nodding-pump-arm` (an arm that nods on its own cycle: you ride the nod), KOH-03 `helper-launched-by-your-jump` (a seesaw you power), CHP-03 `churn-reverses-on-rope-pull`. Different: nobody rides anything; the answer is the still point of a lever, and the tempo comes from a visible body count (one woman, then two), not from a drum.
- **Seconds:** 18-25 (three dhekis, one pattern change).
- **Line:** "Dheki-t bhori nidiba, Dad!" | "Don't put your foot in the dheki, Dad!" (U, my Assamese; fallback Hindi "Dheki mein pair nahi!"). "Lahe lahe" is already Tara's lie in 07, so do not reuse it here.
- **Archetype:** B16 inverted (contact is not a ride), B35 (the fix meets a second trap).
- **Source:** [Wikipedia: Dheki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dheki) (lever length, fulcrum at 5/8, falls by its own weight, 2-3 operators, pitha flour).

### 2. Saraighat-or duta mohola (Saraighat's two decks) — slot 07, the SHELTER beat
- **Place:** the old Saraighat bridge, "a rail-cum-road bridge", 1,298 m, road 7.3 m wide, opened 1962, 40 ft above normal flood level ([Wikipedia: Saraighat Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saraighat_Bridge)). The road runs on the upper deck, the rail on the lower one (the bible, section 2: "steel truss, train below, road above"; deck order U from the page, which only says rail-cum-road).
- **Rule:** the Bordoisila wind of 07 blows across the road deck toward the river rail; the rail deck below is out of the wind. A train uses the rail deck: a hum-shimmer runs along the rails (visual twin of the sound, as 08's dust plume twins the engine) three seconds before it arrives, and the train fills the lower deck for four seconds. No riding, no doors: the train is weather that uses one deck.
- **Decision:** Which deck is the shelter *now* is read from the rails, not from the wind.
- **Obvious play:** 07 taught "shelter under the bhoral when the wind comes", so the player ducks down the pier ladder onto the still rail deck and stays: the rails shimmer, the train comes (`dis-rail`).
- **Obvious fix:** stay up top and brace in the wind as in the open at the bhoral: the gust pushes Dad over the river rail (`dis-bordoisila`, the existing cause, at a different tick and place; if the builder wants a fresh cause, make it the hail on the open deck, `dis-hail`).
- **One-move answer:** down when the rails are dull, up the next pier ladder when they shimmer; the ladders are every bay, so there is always one within a run.
- **Closest:** SVP-08 `rain-direction-reverses-awnings` (the dry side switches with the weather, one shelter kind), B36 shelter trap (TVM), BOM-01/03/05 trains (ride, shove, doors), CHN-08 `s-lifting-bridge-train-roof` (ride the roof). Different: two stacked shelters, each safe from one hazard and open to the other; the train is never boarded or dodged on foot, it only decides which deck.
- **Seconds:** 20-28 (two switches, one false shimmer that is a goods wagon parked and silent, which is B5's liar shown honestly: parked wagons have no shimmer).
- **Line:** "Tolot rail, uporot rasta." | "Rail below, road above." (U; fallback Hindi "Neeche rail, upar sadak.").
- **Archetype:** B36 (shelter trap) with B37's one stable mark (the shimmer).
- **Source:** [Saraighat Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saraighat_Bridge). Deck order and the hum-before-train are U (fallback: a red lamp at the pier that lights 3 s before, the same decision).

### 3. Time card (Kaziranga-or xomoy card) — slot 07, a second SHELTER beat (a wait with a reason); 08 alternate
- **Place:** the highway through Kaziranga at the village's edge. Vehicles get a time card at the entry gate and may not reach the exit gate before the printed time, so they cannot speed where animals cross (U: known Assam forest-department practice, not fetched; fallback: a plain speed-check gate with the same "not before" rule).
- **Rule:** Dad is handed a card at the gate: "exit not before 0:40". The exit gate stays shut to anyone who arrives early and the stakes on it are the 07 gate's (`dis-gate`). Between the gates, animals cross at marked corridors on a cycle; the only place the time can be spent without being on a corridor is the lay-by (a bamboo bench under a shade tree).
- **Decision:** This is a floor, not a deadline: arrive no earlier than the card says, and spend the time in the one place that is off every corridor.
- **Obvious play:** run to the gate as a platformer teaches; it is shut; the player stands at it and the corridor there (a hog deer crossing) runs him down (`dis-horin`, deer; no rhino, 08 owns the rhino and 10 may call it back).
- **Obvious fix:** dawdle in the open, walking slowly: a corridor is where he happens to be when its cycle fires (a different crossing, different tick). The card ticks visibly, so the player sees he was on time and still died: the lesson is *where* to wait.
- **One-move answer:** walk to the lay-by, sit until the card reads 0:00, walk to the gate.
- **Closest:** GTK-05 `dropped-litter-warden-pause` (a pause a warden forces), BLR-02 `s-green-man-signal` (wait for a signal), RPR-03 `s-clock-time-only-when-moving` (time advances only while moving), LKO-07 / AGT-08 pace behind a group, CCU-R `safe-call-window`. Different: nothing paces you and no signal is watched; a printed minimum, in your hand from the start (knowable, TROLL-BIBLE D1), and the trap is that a platformer player has never been punished for being fast.
- **Seconds:** 30-40 including the wait itself (counts as the marathon's rest; put a hidden checkpoint on the bench so the replay from a corridor death is under 8 s).
- **Line:** "Aagote nahiba, Dad. Card-ot likha ase." | "Don't come early, Dad. It's written on the card." (U; fallback "Card dekho, Dad! | Read the card, Dad!" Hindi).
- **Archetype:** B6 inverted (a deadline you must not beat; B6's deadline collapse is FREE and this is its mirror), B38 (Tara urges "run!").
- **Source:** U (no page fetched inside the budget). The builder should open any news report on Kaziranga NH-37 time cards before build; if none holds up, use the speed-gate fallback, which needs no real-world claim.

### 4. Bah-or porcupine (The bamboo spurs) — slot 06 primary, 07 momentum alternate
- **Place:** Majuli's eroding bank of DIS-06. Villagers and the flood department lay "porcupines", stars of bamboo (or concrete) poles, along eroding Brahmaputra banks to slow the current and trap silt (U: standard Assam river-training practice, not fetched; fallback: a row of stone spurs, same physics).
- **Rule:** the 06 slab clock still runs, but behind each spur (downstream, where the drawn current lines bend away) the bank stops going and silt builds a new sand step tile by tile; in front of each spur (upstream) scour speeds the slabs up. Current lines are drawn on the water, so "downstream" is readable.
- **Decision:** Cross on the downstream side of each spur, where the floor grows, never on the upstream side, where it goes faster than 06 taught.
- **Obvious play:** 06 taught "cross at a run": the player runs the straight line past the spurs, which puts him upstream of the next spur, where the slab goes early (`dis-bank`, at a new tick).
- **Obvious fix:** stop on the spur itself (it looks like a solid thing): the star is poles, not a floor; Dad slides between them into the river (`dis-porcupine`, a different trap).
- **One-move answer:** at each spur, step to its downstream side and wait one beat: a sand step rises there; go.
- **Closest:** DIS-06 own slab clock, DIS-02 level post, SML-07 `own-steps-pack-the-snow` (your steps build floor), DDN-R `mineral-crust-grows`, JMU-06 `dam-redirects-water`, LEH-01 `water-turn-switches-channel`. Different: the player neither builds nor redirects anything; a fixed object and a drawn current decide where ground is added and where it is removed, and the sign of it (up or down of the spur) is the whole read.
- **Seconds:** 20-30 in 06 (three spurs, one with a false "downstream" because a side channel joins, drawn as a second current line: that is the knowable twist, not a liar). In 07 it would be a momentum beat (the slabs never stop), but 06 needs it more.
- **Line:** "Porcupine-r pisofale thiya diya." | "Stand behind the porcupine." (U; fallback "Khuta-r pisot | Behind the post", U).
- **Archetype:** B8 (shrinking supports, FREE) with growth on one side; B2 (floor drops ahead) only as the upstream half.
- **Source:** U. Physics of spurs trapping silt downstream is textbook river engineering; the Assam porcupine practice needs one page before build.

### 5. Bank-or bhitor (The inside of the bend) — slot 06, second act
- **Place:** a bend of the Brahmaputra channel at Majuli, with a sandy point bar on the inside and a steep cut bank on the outside.
- **Rule:** a river eats its outer bank at a bend and lays sand on the inner bank. In the stage: on the outside of the drawn curve the slabs fall (06's rule); on the inside, sand steps rise toward the water at the same clock. No post, no spur: the curve on the backdrop is the only tell.
- **Decision:** At a bend, leave the outer bank and cross to the inner bar, because that is where the ground is coming, not going.
- **Obvious play:** keep to the bank you are on (the outside, nearest the door): the slabs go faster into the bend (`dis-bank`).
- **Obvious fix:** jump for the far bank straight across at the apex: that is the deepest water (the pool sits at the outer bend) and the far side at the apex is also outer-bank for the next bend; the hop lands on a slab already cracked (`dis-bank`, different place; or give it `dis-luit` to vary the cause).
- **One-move answer:** cross *before* the bend on the straight, land on the inside, walk the growing bar round.
- **Closest:** DIS-06 own, candidate 4 (same family: build 4 or 5 in 06, not both, unless one is a room and one an act with a stated difference), SVP-09 `brick-crumbles-roots-hold`, TVM-04 shyLedge. Different: nothing retreats from you and nothing holds; which side lives is a fact about the river's shape.
- **Seconds:** 15-25.
- **Line:** "Bhitor-or paar-ot zaok." | "Go to the inner bank." (U; fallback "Andar wale kinare | Inner bank", Hindi).
- **Archetype:** B37 decoy twin (two banks drawn alike, the curve is the stable mark).
- **Source:** cut bank / point bar is standard geomorphology (Wikipedia "Meander"); not fetched inside the budget, so U-until-opened. Majuli itself is V ([Wikipedia: Majuli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majuli), via the bible).

### 6. Kah-or thal (The bell-metal disc) — slot 09 primary (tough, knowable)
- **Place:** a Sarthebari bell-metal yard shown inside the Rang Ghar fair (bell metal of Sarthebari is in the bible, section 3, unverified; brass is RNC's, this is bell metal, and the mechanic is the hammering, not the metal).
- **Rule:** a hot disc lies on an anvil; three smiths stand round it and strike the rim in turn; after each strike the disc indexes a notch, so the three strike points walk round the rim. The centre never gets struck. Every twelve strikes the disc is lifted by tongs and quenched: steam over the whole anvil for one second (the tongs are the tell, raised a beat before).
- **Decision:** On a thing being worked by several people, stand where none of them works (the centre), and leave it only when the tongs rise.
- **Obvious play:** hop across the disc rim to rim: a hammer lands on the landing (`dis-hatur`, hammer).
- **Obvious fix:** stand on the rim between two smiths and shuffle round away from the hammers: the disc indexes under him into the next strike point (`dis-hatur` again would be the same cause: give this one the quench, `dis-bhap`, by timing: the shuffler is still on the disc when the tongs rise).
- **One-move answer:** centre, three strikes, tongs rise, step off to the anvil's foot, wait out the steam, on.
- **Closest:** CHH-03 `potter-lifts-then-pinches` (a maker reshapes what you stand on), AMR-09 `s-lathe-shrinks-radius`, candidate 1 (same still-point answer: build the dheki in 07 and this in 09 only if the builder makes the quench the 09 ladder's A3; otherwise keep one). Different from CHH-03: the shape does not change under you; the hazard is where the makers' hands go, and the pattern is three points walking round a circle.
- **Seconds:** 20-25 (two discs, the second with four smiths, so the still point is the same but the gaps are shorter: a ladder A2 by count).
- **Line:** "Mazkhan-ot thiya ho, Dad." | "Stand in the middle, Dad." (U; fallback "Beech mein, Dad!").
- **Archetype:** B12 (wave: the strike point walks) used as a pattern you stand outside of, B35 (quench on the fix).
- **Source:** Sarthebari bell metal U (bible marks it unverified; no page fetched). The hammering-in-turns detail is my reconstruction: before build, open one page on Sarthebari kahar craft or change the smiths to one smith whose hammer walks round the rim, which keeps the decision.

### 7. Xoru itar khoz (The thin-brick steps) — slot 09, a ladder beat using 09's own ball rule
- **Place:** the Rang Ghar's side wall. The Ahoms "used special, thin, baked bricks" and a rice-and-egg paste as mortar ([Wikipedia: Rang Ghar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rang_Ghar)); the roof is "shaped like an inverted royal Ahom long boat" with a pair of carved stone crocodiles on top (same source).
- **Rule:** the wall's thin courses step back by half a tile each: the whole wall is walkable without a jump. 09 taught that landing on the oiled roof shakes the gutter and drops the wooden ball (`dis-roof`); the gutter runs along this wall too, and any *landing* (a jump's end) on the courses shakes it.
- **Decision:** Walk up what looks like it needs jumping; here the jump is the thing that kills.
- **Obvious play:** hop up the courses as any stair: the first landing drops the ball (`dis-roof`).
- **Obvious fix:** one big run-jump to the top: the stone crocodile finial's snout is where that jump's arc peaks (`dis-kumbhir`, crocodile, a plain visible hazard).
- **One-move answer:** walk; the half-tile courses carry Dad up with no input but right.
- **Closest:** B10 invisible bridge (door `invisible` taken, floor FREE), 09's own roof rule, CHN-10 `stair-direction-by-load`, DIS-09 `dis09-chaal` grip pads. Different: it is a within-stage ladder (TROLL-BIBLE C), not a new type: the stage's own countermeasure (land softly on pads) is turned into "do not land at all". It is here because 09 is the tough-knowable slot and needs beats whose A2/A3 are readable.
- **Seconds:** 12-18.
- **Line:** "Zop nidiba, khoj diya." | "Don't jump, step." (U; fallback "Chhalang nahi, chalo." Hindi).
- **Archetype:** B10 (walkable "gap"), B14 inverted (the hop is the hazard, the walk is clear).
- **Source:** [Rang Ghar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rang_Ghar) (thin bricks, boat roof, crocodiles, games at Rupahi Pathar).

### 8. Xorai-r dhakoni (The xorai lid) — slot 05, a readability beat for the gamosa rule, 12-15 s
- **Place:** the welcome yard of DIS-05. A xorai is the Assamese bell-metal offering tray on a stem, with a domed lid, used to present tamul-paan (betel) and the gamosa to a guest (U detail: lid use at welcome; the object is V as a common Assamese artefact, no page fetched).
- **Rule:** every host has a xorai at his feet. Lid ON: he has not served yet, so he is a *giver* (he will put a gamosa on you). Lid OFF: he has served, he is a *taker* (he bows for one back). The 05 rule itself (three gamosas hide your feet; give one away by walking into a taker) stays as built.
- **Decision:** Read the lid before you reach the host: it tells you whether this contact adds or removes a gamosa.
- **Obvious play / fix / answer:** as 05's own acts; what changes is that the fourth-gamosa blind (10's callback) and the pit become knowable one host earlier. This is TROLL-BIBLE D1 (cause visible at respawn) applied to 05's "how would I know", not a new family.

- **Seconds:** 12-15 as a yard of five hosts whose lids are mixed; worth it only because 05 is 32 s short and its loom is leaving.
- **Line:** "Dhakoni khula? Teonte lobo." | "Lid open? Then he'll take." (U; fallback "Dhakkan khula hai? Woh lega." Hindi).
- **Archetype:** B37 decoy twin, honest (one stable mark).
- **Source:** U (xorai and tamul-paan are everyday Assam; a tourism page would confirm the lid).

### F1. Ghat-or pontoon (The ferry gangway) — breaks 02's level-post rule
- **Rule taught (02):** when the post rises a mark, the sandbars move; what you stand on goes, the new bar rises.
- **The break:** the finale's post stands on the far bank (the allocated twist). At the Fancy Bazar ghat a ferry is moored to a floating pontoon, reached from the stone ghat steps by a hinged gangway. The post rises: the pontoon and gangway *stay* (they float), the fixed ghat steps go under one by one, and the gangway's far end tilts up as the bank end drowns. Nobody boards the ferry; it is the mooring, not a ride.
- **Decision:** What floats stays, what is fixed drowns: the opposite of 02.
- **Obvious play:** 02's answer: step off the thing you are on onto the "new bar" (the next ghat step up): it drowns next (`dis-luit`). **Fix:** run up the ghat to the promenade (the high fixed ground): the promenade rail is where the Bordoisila callback's gust lands if the builder chains them, or make the steps there slick with the river's wet line (`dis-bank`). **Answer:** walk onto the gangway and stand on the pontoon until the post stops, then take the gangway's new angle up to the door on the ferry's *landing stage*, never aboard.
- **Closest:** DIS-02 own; PAT-05 `float-sinks-under-load` (a float sinks because of you: the opposite sign, and nobody loads this pontoon), DMN-05 `s-rope-ferry-mash`, PNJ-01 `s-ferry-poi-tour` (rides). The bible bans the ride, not the ghat.
- **Seconds:** 15-20. **Line:** "Uti thaka bostu-tu-ei thake." | "The floating thing is the one that stays." (U; fallback "Jo tairta hai, wahi rehta hai." Hindi). **Archetype:** B35 (the taught fix is the trap), B37. **Source:** ferries and ghats at Guwahati V as places (bible section 2: Umananda ferry, a 10-minute ride, [Wikipedia: Umananda Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umananda_Island)); pontoon-and-gangway layout at Fancy Bazar ghat U (fallback: a moored country boat tied to a floating bamboo platform, same decision). Umananda itself is not shown: the ferry's destination is off-screen.

### F2. Borokhun-or pisot Rang Ghar (Rang Ghar after rain) — breaks 09's ribs rule
- **Rule taught (09):** the oil slides you; the ribs and the red-and-white grip pads before them are the still places; landing on the oil drops the ball.
- **The break:** it has rained (the finale's Bordoisila callback is its reason). The oil has washed down the gutter; the brick tiles grip. The wooden ribs are wet and are now the slide, and the pads have gone with the water. The gutter is full: landing *anywhere* on the roof now drops the ball, not only on oil.
- **Decision:** The tiles grip, the ribs slide, and nothing may be landed on: walk the whole roof.
- **Obvious play:** 09's answer: hop rib to rib: the rib slides him (`dis-roof` on a rib). **Fix:** stop on a pad: there is none; he is on a rib (same cause: so make the fix "jump the rib onto tile", which lands, and the full gutter drops the ball, `dis-gutter`). **Answer:** walk across the tiles and step over each rib without a jump (make the wet ribs half-height so a walk clears them and a jump is never needed).
- **Closest:** DIS-09 own; SVP-IDEAS "stage's own countermeasure made the vulnerability" (TROLL-BIBLE law 2). **Seconds:** 15-20. **Line:** "Tel nai, pani ase." | "No oil, there's water." (U). **Archetype:** B35. **Source:** [Rang Ghar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rang_Ghar) (boat roof, V); the roof's oil and ribs are DIS's fiction.

### F3. Kolom kota (Pruning season) — breaks 03's tea-row rule
- **Rule taught (03):** hedges are taller than a jump; follow the plucker; the hedge she has plucked steps down to one step.
- **The break:** it is cold season and the garden is being pruned: the pruner's hedge is cut to knee height, not stepped down; following her lands you in the drain between the rows (`dis-nala`, the garden drain, a new honest hazard), while the *unpruned* hedge next to it, still tall, has a flat table that is now the floor, because the pruned rows beside it are low enough to climb from.
- **Decision:** Do not follow the worker this time; walk the tall table the worker has not reached.
- **Obvious play:** follow the pruner as in 03 (drain). **Fix:** hop the drain to the next pruned row and follow her there: the pruning shears swing back on her turn (`dis-kesi`, shears; the 03 basket's cousin, different cause). **Answer:** climb onto the unpruned table from the first pruned row and walk it to the door, ahead of her.
- **Closest:** DIS-03 own; IMP-03 `shears-trim-tier` (a tier is trimmed: there the trimming is the hazard you dodge; here the trimming only removes a floor and the answer is to leave the worker). **Seconds:** 15-20. **Line:** "Aji tuluwa nai, kota." | "No plucking today, cutting." (U; fallback "Aaj kaatna hai, todna nahi." Hindi). **Archetype:** B35, B38 (Tara: "follow her!"). **Source:** tea bushes in Assam are pruned in the cold season between plucking rounds (U, not fetched; fallback: "the rows are being uprooted for replanting", same decision).

### F4. Thanda tawa (The pan gone cold) — breaks 04's shine rule
- **Rule taught (04):** shiny = liquid, holds you; matte = set, and the cook rolls it with you inside.
