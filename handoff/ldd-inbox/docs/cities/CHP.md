## 1. Identity

- To the people who live there: the "City Beautiful" in numbered sectors, Punjab's capital (shared with
  Haryana), the calm planned town next to the loud, generous Punjab of fields, dhabas and weddings.
- Visitors come for: the Rock Garden, Sukhna Lake, the Rose Garden, then out to Punjab: Amritsar's food
  lanes, Patiala's juttis and pagris, Ludhiana's markets, Kila Raipur's rural games.
- Proud and laughing: hospitality that will not take no ("lassi pee ke ja"), weddings louder than the
  town, and a city where even the sector map has its jokes (there is no Sector 13) **[L]**
  [Chandigarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandigarh).

## 6. Look and sound

- Palette: sky `#BFD9EE` (clear winter morning), hills `#6E7F5A` (Shivalik scrub), tiles `#C9B79C`
  (Chandigarh's exposed concrete and brick), accent `#F2C230` (mustard bloom), second accent `#D2335A`
  (phulkari magenta).
- Skyline: low flat concrete blocks with brise-soleil grids, a hand-shaped vane on a pole (Open Hand,
  generic), mosaic figures in rows, Shivalik ridge behind; out in Punjab: flat fields, a tubewell hut, trees
  in rows on field edges.
- Ground cap: concrete kerb with a white-painted edge in town; ploughed furrow in the fields.
- Weather: clear dry winter sun; a pale haze at the horizon (not fog).
- Script: Gurmukhi (Punjabi) **[F]** [phrasebook](https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Punjabi_phrasebook).
- Music: 120-130 bpm, bhangra feel on a dhol pattern (chaal), tumbi-like plucked lead, algoza-like double
  flute for calm parts, Mixolydian-flavoured major (unverified tag). Note JAI-02 used dhol(ak) beats as a
  forced-hop mechanic: here the dhol is music only.
- Postcard line: "Kiddan, Chandigarh! | Sectors, sarson, and a very loud wedding."
