## 1. Identity

- To the people who live there: a farming and dairy state of straight talk, milk, ghee and champions, where
  the capital is a shared city and the real life is in the villages and the fast new towns around Delhi.
- Visitors come for: the Surajkund crafts fair, Kurukshetra, Pinjore's gardens, Sultanpur's birds, Rewari's
  steam engines, and the sport (Bhiwani's boxers, the akharas).
- Proud and laughing: "Doodh-dahi ka khaana" (milk-and-curd food) as the state's own boast (unverified
  saying, very common), the blunt Haryanvi answer that sounds like a fight and is a kindness, and the tau
  under the tree who has advice for everyone.

## 4. Language pack (Haryanvi + Hindi + English)

Mix: about 50% Haryanvi, 30% Hindi, 20% English. Haryanvi is a Western Hindi dialect, written in
Devanagari, 9.8 million speakers **[F]** [Haryanvi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haryanvi_language). No
phrasebook could be read in this run, so every Haryanvi line is **U** with a plain-Hindi fallback; a native
speaker must check them. Features used (unverified, widely seen): *se* for "is" (ke haal se), *su* "I am",
*konya* "not", *thaara* "your", *mhara* "our", *ghana* "very", *chhora* "boy", *tau* "uncle", *taai* "aunt",
*kit* "where".

## 6. Look and sound

- Palette: sky `#E8D9B0` (dusty bright summer), hills `#A08C6A` (Aravalli scrub, low), tiles `#B5553A`
  (red akhara earth), accent `#1E1E1E` (Murrah black), second accent `#F4F1E6` (white butter).
- Skyline: flat fields, a peepal-like tree with a charpai, mud-and-brick houses with flat roofs, a water
  tank on stilts, a brick loco shed, a few glass towers far off (generic, no brands).
- Ground cap: packed earth with a cart rut; red raked earth in the akhara.
- Weather: hard sun, a dust haze; no rain.
- Script: Devanagari **[F]** [Haryanvi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haryanvi_language).
- Music: 110-120 bpm, folk feel of dhol and the been/chimta-like jingle (unverified instruments; check
  [Music of Haryana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Haryana)), a sung-call phrase answered by a
  second voice (ragini feel), Bhairavi-like minor (unverified tag). Keep the dhol pattern different from
  CHP's bhangra chaal.
- Postcard line: "Ke haal se? | Milk, mud and very straight talk."
