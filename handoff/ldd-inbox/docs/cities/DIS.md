# DIS · Dispur (Guwahati), Assam: city bible

Research for Level Devil Dad, chapter 22. Dispur is the capital, inside Guwahati; stages are set in Guwahati and
across Assam. Facts carry a link or say (unverified). Checked against `docs/MECHANICS.md` and the Done tables.
Taken and left alone: floods and rising water (TVM-02, BOM-04), boats and ferries (bhutbhuti rides), `whirlwind`
(so no "Brahmaputra eddy" spin), river dolphins (KVT), golden silk from cocoons (RPR-06 kosa; Sualkuchi looms also
stay out because BBI took the loom with ikat), elephants and jeep safaris (JAI-08, `safari`), pot-breaking on
height (`dahihandi`). Hooks DIS holds in this batch: the river's moving sandbars, tea gardens (given here, not to
GTK), Bihu (dhol, pepa, husori carol groups, given here, not to GTK's Deusi), Bhogali Bihu's bhelaghar feast huts,
gamosa, japi, the one-horned rhino, Majuli's shrinking banks, the Ahom pavilion and the palace with floors
underground, til pitha, lal chah.

## 1. Identity
- To Guwahatians: the gateway to the Northeast on the widest river you'll ever see, the Luit; a city of hills,
  flyovers and lal chah, that stops for Bihu in April and eats for Bhogali in January.
- Visitors come for: the Brahmaputra riverfront and Umananda, Fancy Bazar, Kaziranga's rhinos, Majuli, the tea
  estates of Upper Assam, Sivasagar's Ahom monuments.
- Proud and laughing at: "Assam tea for breakfast, lunch and dinner", the gamosa that's a towel, a scarf, a gift
  and a bandana, and the uncle who says "bhal" to everything.

## 3. Everyday life hooks
- **Transport.** City buses, shared autos and e-rickshaws on GS Road (generic: avoid); ferries (taken); the
  riverfront promenade walk.
- **Food.** Til pitha (thin rice-flour crepe with sesame-jaggery, rolled on a hot pan), masor tenga (sour fish
  curry), khar (alkaline dish), aloo pitika (mashed potato with mustard oil), laru (coconut or sesame balls),
  lal chah (black tea) ([Magh Bihu wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magh_Bihu) for pitha and laru; the rest
  unverified).
  - Hidden-item candidates: **til pitha** (first choice), **laru**, **lal chah**.
  - Traps: til pitha: the crepe is rolled as soon as it sets: you on it get rolled up and dropped out of
    the end; laru: rolled balls that roll away when stepped on (rolling = JAI-05 bangle: careful, use only as
    a hazard, never as a ride); lal chah: a tray of cups that the tea boy carries through a crowd.
- **Markets.** Fancy Bazar, Paltan Bazar, Ganeshguri (unverified).
- **Weather.** Pre-monsoon storms in April (Bohag), long monsoon, floods (taken), winter fog on the river.
- **Animals.** One-horned rhino (Kaziranga), hoolock gibbons (unverified), golden langurs (no longer on
  Umananda), river dolphins (taken).
- **Crafts.** Gamosa (white cotton with a red border, given as honour), japi (big conical palm-leaf and bamboo
  hat), bell metal of Sarthebari (unverified; brass is RNC's), bamboo and cane, muga silk (taken by RPR's kosa).
- **Sports and games.** Football and cricket (taken); tekeli bhonga (pot-breaking game at Magh Bihu,
  [wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magh_Bihu)); buffalo fights (**avoid**: animal welfare and court cases).
- **Festivals.**
  - Rongali/Bohag Bihu (mid-April): Bihu dance, dhol, the **pepa**, a buffalo-horn pipe blown at Bihu since
    ancient times ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pepa_(instrument))), husori groups singing house to
    house (CITIES.md; unverified details).
  - Magh/Bhogali Bihu (14-15 Jan): Uruka night feast, group fishing, **bhelaghar** huts and **meji** bonfires of
    bamboo, leaves and thatch built by young people, slept in or feasted in, and burned next morning; pitha
    and laru made ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magh_Bihu)). The meji fire is a prayer: use the
    *building* of the bhelaghar and the feast, not the burning.
- **Habits and sayings.** "Kenekua aase?", "Bhal" (good), "Ki khobor?" (also Assamese, unverified spelling
  "ki khobor"), gamosa round the neck, tea at every visit.

## 5. Twelve stage concepts
**1. Nomoskar Guwahati** · tour · Riverfront, flyovers, the river's sandbars in the backdrop. Betrayal: a tea boy
with a tray of lal chah walks the promenade against you; touch the tray and it spills hot tea on the tile you
were landing on. Second try: let him pass on the river side. Closest: TVM-03's tea pour (starts as you leave the
steam); different: a moving carrier and a spill you cause by contact.

**2. Char (The sandbar)** · standard · Brahmaputra sandbars. Expect: sand islands are solid. Betrayal: there is a
river-level post; at each mark the sandbars are in different places: the one at "2" is gone at "3", and a new
one rises downstream. The level changes when the dam upstream (backdrop siren) releases. Second try: watch the
post, step on the bar the next mark makes. FIX: the dad who reads the post waits on a bar that's at both marks
but shrinks. Act: three posts, three channels. Door: the char where a farmer's hut is, which never moves (the
farmer knows). Closest: rising water (floods), tides (DMN, PDY-06), KVT-08's sandbar that goes under when you
stop; different: bars relocate by a readable level, not sink by your stopping.

**3. Majuli ka Paar (Majuli's bank)** · long · The eroding bank. Expect: the river rises. Betrayal: the river
doesn't rise; the bank retreats: the edge crumbles into the river in slabs, always the slab you are standing
nearest the water on; walk inland and the far bank you need gets farther. Second try: cross the slabs at a run,
never stand at the edge. Second betrayal: a stilt house's ladder is pulled up when the owner sees you coming.
Door: the stilt house door, reached by its ladder at the moment it's let down for the tea boy. Closest:
crumbling floors, TVM-04's backing-away bank (`shyLedge`); different: the land loses ground in slabs toward
you, by distance to water.

**4. Duta Paat, Ekta Kuhi (Two leaves and a bud)** · standard · Tea garden. Expect: tea bushes as hedges. Betrayal:
the flat-topped bushes are the floor; pluckers walk the rows and pluck the top; a plucked bush is a few pixels
lower, and a row that's been plucked is a step down you didn't plan for; unplucked rows have buds (spikes? No:
buds are soft; the plucker's basket is the hazard, swinging as she turns). Second try: walk behind a plucker.
Second betrayal: the shade trees' shadow? (taken) No: the sprinkler line at noon wets the path. Act: the factory:
leaves rolled under rollers (rollers in kit: keep only the withering racks, trays that tilt to drain). Door:
the tea chest, nailed shut once full. Closest: JAI-03 pots, kit rollers; different: the floor is the crop.

**5. Pepa (The buffalo-horn pipe)** · standard · Bohag Bihu stage. Expect: a dance beat (JAI-02 is taken).
Betrayal: when the pepa sounds, every dancer drops low (the Bihu dip) and the platforms on their japi hats drop
with them; when the dhol takes over, they rise. Second try: stand on hats on the dhol, step off before the pepa.
Second betrayal: a second pepa player at the back starts early. Door: the dhol itself: jump on its face on the
last beat. Closest: GNR-07 rings, RNC-05 masks; different: two instruments, each with its own body movement.

**6. Husori (The carollers)** · marathon · House-to-house singing. Expect: a crowd in the way. Betrayal: the husori
group stops at every gate, and the gate opens only for them; you must be inside the group (walk at their pace,
inside the circle) to get through each gate. Second try: join them. Second betrayal: the host gives gamosas to
the group and the group stops to receive them; leaving early shuts you out. FIX: the dad who stays too long is
handed a gamosa and must carry it (no jumping with both hands full? Keep simple: carrying it means a short jump).
Door: the last house's courtyard. Closest: DMN-04 plank passed hand to hand, crowds; different: you must be part
of a moving group, and the group's ritual is the timer.

**7. Bhelaghar (The feast hut)** · long · Uruka night. Expect: a hut to shelter in. Betrayal: young people build
the hut from bamboo, leaves and thatch as you climb it; the bamboo frame is solid, the thatch is not, and they
lay the thatch in rows from the bottom up, covering your footholds. Second try: climb ahead of the thatch, on the
frame. Second betrayal: Uruka night group fishing: the net is spread across the pond you need, people pull it
in as one. Door: the feast inside: the door opens when the pitha is served. Respect note: the burning at dawn is
never shown as a trap. Closest: CCU-09 pandal cloth panels (same family! keep only one: give the "cover goes up
where your foothold was" rule to one city; recommend DIS keeps it if CCU builds Kumartuli instead).

**8. Gorh (The rhino)** · "how would I know" · Kaziranga grassland. Expect: a charging animal to dodge (chasers
exist). Betrayal: the rhino doesn't care about you; it charges the jeep's engine noise (backdrop), and the grass
it flattens on its run is the only path through. Second try: wait for the engine, follow the flattened strip.
Second betrayal: a second rhino charges the other way along a different strip. Door: the watch tower's ladder.
Closest: GNR-05 lions, bull ending, chasers; different: you use its path, it targets something else, and the
grass changes shape.

**9. Til Pitha (The rolled crepe)** · standard · A pitha pan. Expect: a hot pan to avoid. Betrayal: crepes set
in a ring and the cook rolls each one from the edge as soon as it's set; standing on a set crepe rolls you up
and drops you out of the end, back where you came. Second try: stand on a crepe that's still liquid-shiny (not
set); it holds you warm but unrolled. Door: the laru plate. Closest: RNC-02 dhuska (flipped and scooped);
different: rolled from an edge toward you, readable by shine.

**10. Rang Ghar (The Ahom pavilion)** · long · Sivasagar. Expect: the courtyard is the floor. Betrayal: the
courtyard is a game ground where royal games run (no buffalo fights: use wrestlers or a tug of war); the boat
roof of the pavilion is the safest floor, and the stairs to it are behind the crowd. Second try: climb the
pavilion's side. Second betrayal: the roof's curve slides you down unless you stop at its ribs. Door: the royal
seat. Note: verify Rang Ghar details before build.

**11. Gamosa (The cloth)** · "how would I know" · A welcome. Expect: a gift is a reward. Betrayal: every host
puts a gamosa round your neck; with three gamosas Dad can't see his feet (the camera hides the floor under
him) and walks off a ledge; you must give gamosas away to the next person who bows. Second try: bow back (stand
still) to give one away. Door: the last gamosa is the door's latch rope. Closest: LKO-02 pehle aap (bowing too:
make sure only one city uses "stand still to bow"; give it to LKO, and here make the give-away a walk-into-them
contact instead).

**12. Luit (The river, finale)** · finale · Callbacks only to DIS: the char that moves with the level post, the
Majuli bank that slabs away, the tea rows that drop when plucked, the pepa dip and dhol rise, the husori group
gate, the rolled pitha. New twist: the level post is on the tea estate's far bank, and only pluckers' paths go
there.

Picks: 1 + 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12. (7 and 11 are reserves until CCU-09 and LKO-02 are decided.)

## 6. Look and sound
- Palette: sky `#D6E4D9` (humid pale green-grey), hills `#4F7942` (tea and forest), tiles `#A9927D` (river sand), accent
  `#C1121F` (gamosa red), second accent `#FDF0D5` (gamosa white), river `#8D7B68`.
- Skyline: low hills, the wide river with sand islands, a steel truss bridge, flyovers, tea rows on far hills,
  a boat-roofed pavilion for Sivasagar stages. Ground cap: tea-bush tops for garden stages, riverbank sand with a
  bamboo fence for the city.
- Weather: April storm clouds, heavy monsoon, winter fog on the river at dawn.
- Local script name: গুৱাহাটী (Assamese; Dispur: দিছপুৰ, unverified spelling).
- Music: 120 bpm, Bihu feel: dhol (two-headed drum, played with stick and hand) on a driving Bihu beat, pepa
  (buffalo-horn pipe) lead, gogona (bamboo jaw harp, unverified) and taal cymbals; major pentatonic.
- Postcard line: "The river moves the islands. The dhol moves everyone else."

## 7. Avoid (hard no)
Insurgency and armed groups; citizenship and migration politics (NRC, "outsiders"); ethnic tension between
communities; floods as comedy; Kamakhya, Umananda's temple, satras and any religious site as a trap; the meji fire
as a trap; buffalo fights; rhino poaching; real tea companies and brands; Jatinga bird deaths; comparing Assam to
other Northeast states; look-based slurs.
