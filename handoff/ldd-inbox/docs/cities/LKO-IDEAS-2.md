### Stage 07 Hazratganj (marathon): needs about 69 s

#### 7.1 Kohra (The fog) — the MOMENTUM beat
- **Place:** the colonnade's far half at dusk, the Gomti end. Fog is common in Lucknow from mid-December to late January ([Wikipedia: Lucknow, Climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucknow)); the bible's 07 is a winter evening stroll.
- **Rule:** a fog bank rolls in from behind at a speed between the strollers' pace and a pause (it never catches a walker who does not stop). Inside the fog the nalas are not drawn; only the lamp posts' pools of light show the floor. Along the way, gentlemen on chabutras greet you ("Aadaab").
- **Decision:** In the fog's reach, do not return a greeting; manners wait.
- **Obvious play:** stop for the short bow 02 taught (`lko02-aadaab`): the fog reaches you, the next nala is grey, you walk into it. Death line idea: "Kohra! Aadaab ka waqt nahin tha."
- **Obvious fix:** run ahead of the fog: the strollers close ranks and tut you into the nala (`lko-ganj`, the room's rule, honest and visible).
- **One-move answer:** walk at stroller pace and step round the greeter without stopping; the greeter does not shove a walker who is already past the chabutra (unlike 02, the shove needs you to stop beside him).
- **Closest:** `lko07-shaam` (pause under the awnings dies), B17 chaser (PARTLY), B12 wave (FREE). Different: shaam punishes any pause; kohra punishes one specific polite stop the city taught you to make, so 02's rule goes stale inside 07 (law 6) and the finale's theme (manners vs survival) is planted. The fog is slow, visible and never faster than a walk.
- **Time:** 18-25 s (three greeters, the third on the far side of a nala so the step-round needs the lamp's light).
- **Line:** "Kohre mein aadaab baad mein, janab." | "In the fog, greetings later, sir." (U, standard Hindustani; fallback "Rukiye mat!" | "Don't stop!").
- **Archetype:** law 2 (countermeasure becomes vulnerability), B12 shape.

#### 7.2 Chhidkav (The evening sprinkle) — the SHELTER beat
- **Place:** shopfronts under the arcade; shop boys fling a bucket of water across the pavement at dusk to settle the dust (everyday north-Indian habit; U as a Hazratganj fact).
- **Rule:** every 4 s a boy at a shop door flings an arc from the SHOP side outward across the pavement to the kerb; the arcade pillars are solid; a pillar blocks the arc on its far face only. The third shop's boy is rinsing his bucket at the kerb and flings INWARD.
- **Decision:** Put the pillar between you and the thrower, reading where he stands, not which side "feels" sheltered.
- **Obvious play:** hug the shopfront (the inner side, away from the strollers): the arc comes from the shop door and knocks you off the kerb into the nala.
- **Obvious fix:** walk the kerb edge instead: the third boy throws from the kerb inward (he is drawn at the kerb with his bucket), different cause (`lko-balti-ulti`).
- **One-move answer:** stand on the pillar's face away from the boy for one arc, then go; the arc is 1.1 s, the gap 2.9 s.
- **Closest:** PAT-04 step aside for an oncoming load (taken), `lko07-saamne` (cut). Different: nothing comes down the pavement at you and there is no alcove to enter; the hazard comes from the side and the choice is which face of a fixed post, which flips when the thrower's side flips.
- **Time:** 12-15 s (three shops).
- **Line:** "Khambe ke peeche, janab, paani aa raha hai." | "Behind the pillar, sir, water's coming." (U).
- **Archetype:** B36 shelter trap only in shape; the shelter is honest, the side is the puzzle.

#### 7.3 Sheeshe ki dukaan (The window-shopper)
- **Place:** showroom windows under the arcade (Hazratganj shops sell chikan; Victorian-style shopping area, [Wikipedia: Hazratganj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazratganj)).
- **Rule:** a stroller stands at a lit window; when a new item lights in the display (every 3 s) he steps BACK one tile to take it in, then steps forward again. The gap between him and the glass is one Dad wide.
- **Decision:** Pass a window-shopper on the window side, not the open side.
- **Obvious play:** go round him on the open pavement side: he steps back into you, off the kerb into the nala (`lko-sheesha`).
- **Obvious fix:** pause until he is done (the room taught that strollers part for a pause): a window-shopper never parts; the awnings above are the dusk awnings (`lko-shamiyana`, the stage's standing hazard, so the fix dies to a known cause: acceptable, as FIX-2 did with chauraha).
- **One-move answer:** walk the one-tile gap along the glass, between him and the window.
- **Closest:** SVP-IDEAS-2 candidate 10 (which side of a post with a trailing bag; unbuilt, other city, a tether rule). Different: the thing you pass moves toward the open side on a visible cue; no tether, no post.
- **Time:** 8-10 s (two windows; the second shopper is a parent lifting a child, who steps back twice).
- **Line:** "Sheeshe ki taraf se, janab." | "Along the glass, sir." (U).
- **Archetype:** B3 semantic reversal (the open side is the dangerous side).

#### 7.4 Rickshaw ki chhat (The rickshaw hood) — alternate to 7.3, same decision class
- **Place:** a cycle-rickshaw stand at the GPO corner (U: rickshaws inside the makeover zone).
- **Rule:** when a passenger sits, the puller flips the folded hood up and back over the rear in one swing, then pedals off forward. The swing covers the tile behind the rickshaw.
- **Decision:** Pass a boarding rickshaw at its front wheel, not behind it.
- **Obvious play / fix / answer:** pass behind (the hood knocks you into the road, `lko-hood`); pass well in front (the next rickshaw pulls out as its passenger sits, `lko-rickshaw`); pass tight along the front wheel of a rickshaw whose passenger is still standing.
- **Closest:** 7.3 (same class: which side of a person-thing); PAT-10 tempo (a vehicle that leaves when boarded: taken as a ride, here nobody rides). Build 7.3 or 7.4, not both.
- **Time:** 8-10 s. **Line:** "Aage se, peeche chhat girti hai." | "Round the front, the hood comes down behind." (U). **Archetype:** B3.

#### 7.5 Daane wale kabootar (Pigeons at the grain seller) — alternate MOMENTUM beat
- **Place:** the pavement by a grain seller; the builder's own idea, which QA called good (`QA/FAIL.md` B3).
- **Rule:** the flock on the pavement lifts for a walker and lands again behind him; near the grain, a Dad who stands for more than 0.8 s is taken for a feeder and the flock lands on his shoulders and head: Dad is grounded for 2 s (cannot jump), and the nala is one tile on.
- **Decision:** Near grain, never stand; walk through the flock.
- **Obvious play:** pause so the birds part (the room's stroller rule): they land on you; the stroller behind walks into your back and you go into the nala (`lko-daana`).
- **Obvious fix:** rush through: the strollers past the flock tut you (`lko-ganj`).
- **One-move answer:** walk, do not pause, straight through the birds.
- **Closest:** CCU-08 adda / "stand still and someone holds you" (taken). Honest verdict: birds landing on a still Dad is that decision in feathers unless the kill is the stroller behind, not the birds. Lower rank; use 7.1 as the momentum beat and this only if fog is refused (no fog in MECHANICS.md at all; grep count 0).
- **Time:** 8-10 s. **Line:** "Daane ke paas rukiye mat." | "Don't stop by the grain." (U). **Archetype:** law 2.

#### 7.6 Carnival ki raat (The second-Sunday carnival) — a scene change, not a type
- **Place:** the monthly Hazratganj carnival on the second Sunday, with cultural programmes ([Wikipedia: Hazratganj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazratganj)); fountains and the small amphitheatre from the makeover.
- **Rule:** the colourful fountains (makeover, V) jet on a cycle; a stall row narrows the pavement to one lane. This is a readable frame for 7.1-7.3 in the marathon's second half (new backdrop, same decisions), not a new decision: the jets are the `cascade` kit (taken), so do not make them the hazard; draw them behind the playfield.
- **Verdict:** use as the setting of the fog half so the lamps have a reason to be lit and crowded; adds no seconds by itself.

### Stage 08 Bhool Bhulaiya (HWIK + tough): needs about 33 s, maze acts of about 6 s

The stage's one WTF is arch 1 (D3). Every candidate below has its tell drawn before the player reaches it. Real features used: 489 identical doorways and eight chambers at different roof heights (V, [Wikipedia: Bara Imambara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bara_Imambara)); the beamless hall over 15 m tall (V, same page); the whispering gallery, the "1024 ways in" and the light openings are tourist-guide lore I could not fetch (U, see the last section). No religious detail is drawn (bible section 7).

#### 8.1 Roshandan (The light well)
- **Place:** a dark gallery; light enters in slanted beams. Some beams come DOWN from ceiling openings (roshandan); some come UP through holes in the floor from the lit hall below (the maze floor is the hall's roof: V that the maze sits above the chambers).
- **Rule:** in the dark the floor is not drawn; a patch lit from above is floor you can see; a patch lit from below is a hole, and the light on the wall above it widens upward.
- **Decision:** Read which way the light comes, not whether a patch is lit.
- **Obvious play:** walk the lit patches (where you can see): the second one is lit from below, and you fall into the hall (`lko-roshandan`).
- **Obvious fix:** walk only in the dark: a low lintel step (a one-tile sill, drawn as a darker band) trips you into the well drain (`lko-kuan`, the stage's standing hazard).
- **One-move answer:** step on patches whose beam narrows downward (from above); step over patches whose beam widens upward.
- **Closest:** LKO-04 (own) light direction sinks foam; GNR-03 shaft sunbeams. Different from 04: here light direction tells floor from hole, not which foam holds, and it is a callback within the city; different from GNR-03: the beams are not platforms or hazards, only information.
- **Time:** 6-8 s (four patches: above, below, above, below-then-above twin).
- **Line:** "Neeche se roshni, neeche hi raasta." | "Light from below, the way is below." (U).
- **Archetype:** B3 semantic reversal (lit = hole).

#### 8.2 Toota mehrab (The broken arch) — a ladder beat, law 6
- **Place:** a row of three arches, the middle one with its crown missing (a gap in the cusp, drawn 3 tiles wide so it reads at phone size).
- **Rule:** the maze rule says jump through = a floor up. An arch without its crown is not an arch: a jumper through it lands on the same floor.
- **Decision:** An arch must be whole to lift you; skip the broken one and jump the next.
- **Obvious play:** jump through as taught: no lift, and the floor beyond ends at the well (`lko-kuan`).
- **Obvious fix:** walk through, as the FIX of the room taught, then walk on: beyond the broken arch the gallery slopes to the hook roof's underside and the hooks catch you (`lko-bhool`), a different cause.
- **One-move answer:** walk past the broken arch and jump through the whole one after it.
- **Closest:** LKO's own room; B37 decoy twin (USED: differ by one stable mark). Honest: this is a within-stage ladder (TROLL-BIBLE C), knowable, not a new type; it is here because 08 is HWIK and its padding must be knowable.
- **Time:** 6 s. **Line:** "Bina sire ka mehrab, mehrab nahin." | "An arch with no crown is no arch." (U). **Archetype:** law 6 (the rule goes stale).

#### 8.3 Sargoshi (The whispering wall)
- **Place:** the curved gallery over the hall; guide lore says a whisper at one end is heard at the other (U). A chowkidar sits at the far end by an iron grille.
- **Rule:** the curved wall carries any sound made against it to the far end; Dad's footsteps are heard only if he walks within one tile of the wall. When the chowkidar hears steps he lowers the grille for 3 s; the grille is the way on, and the gallery floor behind you is being swept by the well's drain overflow (a slow rising line, visible).
- **Decision:** Keep off the wall; walk down the middle of the gallery even though the wall side has the handrail.
- **Obvious play:** walk the wall side (the rail, the "safe" edge): the grille drops and the overflow reaches you (`lko-sargoshi`).
- **Obvious fix:** run the middle: the strip in the middle has the lower-ceiling arch at its end and a runner's jump hits it (`lko-chhat`, the allocation's own A3 "one arch has a low ceiling").
- **One-move answer:** walk the middle, walk through the low arch.
- **Closest:** LKO-06 (own) sound draws the dancer; HYD-06 echo (taken). Different from 06: no one comes to you and no strip rings; the sound closes the way ahead, and the variable is your distance from the wall, not where you land. Different from HYD-06: nothing is copied back. Rank mid: QA may read "your sound costs you" as 06 again; if so, cut.
- **Time:** 6-8 s. **Line:** "Deewar sab sunti hai." | "The wall hears everything." (U). **Archetype:** B35 delayed second trap in shape.

#### 8.4 Ulti seedhi (Down to go up)
- **Place:** a stair junction: one stair goes up to a lit doorway, one goes down into the dark. Both are drawn whole; the up-stair's doorway is on the hook roof (hooks visible, the stage's known tell).
- **Rule:** the maze's levels interleave; the passage that climbs starts from the lower stair.
- **Decision:** At a stair junction, go down to go up; read where each stair's doorway opens, not which way its steps point.
- **Obvious play:** take the up-stair: the doorway at its top is the hooks (`lko-bhool`).
- **Obvious fix:** jump through the up-stair's doorway instead (jump = up, as taught): up is the vault's underside, no floor, down the well (`lko-kuan`).
- **One-move answer:** the down-stair, walk, and its passage rises through a plain arch to the door's gallery.
- **Closest:** `lko08-wapas` (direction matters), `backdoor`. Different: no arch is taken backwards and nothing is jumped; the choice is which stair, read from a visible destination.
- **Time:** 6-7 s. **Line:** "Upar jaane ko neeche utariye." | "To go up, go down." (U). **Archetype:** B3.

#### 8.5 Khidki (The window over the hall) — the terrace exit, use once
- **Place:** the gallery's inner openings look down into the beamless hall (V: the hall is over 15 m tall and has no beams; the "arch you can stand on from the top" is the bible's own section 2 idea).
- **Rule:** an opening whose sill is level with the top of a hall arch lets you step on to that arch and walk its crown; an opening over open hall floor is a 15 m drop.
- **Decision:** Leave the gallery by the window that has an arch top under it, which you can see as a curve meeting the sill.
- **Obvious play:** the first window (the door is visible below through it): no arch under it, the drop (`lko-hall`).
- **Obvious fix:** go on along the gallery to a window with the hooks beside it (the stage says hooks mean "up", so avoid them) and take the one without hooks: it opens over the well shaft (`lko-kuan`).
- **One-move answer:** the window whose sill the arch curve touches; walk the crown down to the floor by the door.
- **Closest:** RNC-07 fall rule (fall height kills: taken as a rule; here the fall is one honest hazard, not the stage's rule). Rank low; use only as the last beat before the door if 8.1-8.4 leave a gap.
- **Time:** 6 s. **Line:** "Mehrab ki peeth par chaliye." | "Walk on the arch's back." (U). **Archetype:** B10 shape (a walkable thing that looks like air).

Chain for 08: room → chausar → minar → mehrab → **8.1** → utar → **8.4** → wapas → **8.2** → **8.3** → door. Added 30-35 s.

### Stage 05 Galouti: needs about 24 s

#### 5.1 Ghee ka chhidka (The ghee ladle)
- **Place:** a nameless kababi's tawa (galawati = "thing that melts", fried in ghee: V, [Wikipedia: Tunde ke kabab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galouti_kebab)).
- **Rule:** the cook ladles ghee along a row of heaps. A heap under fresh ghee sizzles (sizzle lines drawn) and renders down to half height by itself for 1.5 s, then crusts back to full height, glossy and still. Weight does nothing to a ghee'd heap.
- **Decision:** Hop while it sizzles; your weight no longer flattens anything here.
- **Obvious play:** step on and wait for the squash, as the whole stage taught: ghee fries your feet (`lko-ghee`).
- **Obvious fix:** hop at once from any glossy heap (the heap "looked low a moment ago"): a crusted heap is full height, and the hop meets the skewer tips (`lko-seekh`).
- **One-move answer:** step on as the ladle passes, hop within the sizzle.
- **Closest:** `lko05-dum` (wait for the second squash), B5 cadence then a liar (FREE). Different: no squash at all; the cook's ladle, not Dad, lowers the heap, and the tell is sizzle vs gloss, not how many times you pressed.
- **Time:** 12-15 s (four heaps, the ladle moving right at walking pace so a pacing player meets each sizzle; the fourth heap gets a double ladle and sizzles twice).
- **Line:** "Jab tak chhan-chhan ho, tab tak kood." | "While it sizzles, hop." (U).
- **Archetype:** B5 shape; law 6 (the squash rule goes stale).

#### 5.2 Seekh ghoomti hai (The skewers turn) — alternate or second beat
- **Place:** the seekh rack beside the lane: the cook turns the skewers a quarter turn every 2 s; tips up for 2 s (deadly), tips flat for 2 s (a hop under them is clear).
- **Decision:** Hop under the skewers the moment they turn flat, not when they have been still a while.
- **Obvious play:** hop when the tips are up but look far (the lane rule): tips. **Fix:** wait one turn, then another to be sure: the flat heap you wait on burns (`lko-tawa`). **Answer:** hop on the turn.
- **Closest:** B11 spike that slides aside (PARTLY: visible slide FREE), `lko05-lane`. Different from the lane: the tips move, the lane does not. Honest: a cycle-timing beat, ordinary; rank below 5.1.
- **Time:** 8-10 s. **Line:** "Seekh palti, ab kood." | "Skewers turned, now hop." (U). **Archetype:** B11.

### Stage 06 Kathak: needs about 28 s

#### 6.1 Tihai (Thrice, then sam)
- **Place:** the mehfil stage. A tihai is a phrase repeated three times, ending on sam, the first beat (V, [Wikipedia: Tihai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tihai)); a dancer's footwork is timed to the tala (V, [Wikipedia: Kathak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathak)).
- **Rule:** the tabla plays a phrase; she stamps it; the strips she stamps ring (as tatkar). The phrase comes three times, with the same strips each time. On the third ending, sam, she stamps so hard that EVERY strip rings, silent ones too, and she spins to whoever stands on one.
- **Decision:** On the third repeat, be off every strip, not just the silent ones.
- **Obvious play:** stand on a silent strip, the stage's rule: sam rings it (`lko-sam-strip`).
- **Obvious fix:** cross during the second rest (the rests between repeats are equal, V): the second rest is where she travels, and her chakkar path crosses the plain boards you chose (`lko-chakkar`).
- **One-move answer:** cross on the first rest, stand on the takht for repeats two and three.
- **Closest:** `lko06-tatkar` (go where she is not tapping), `lko-sam` door (wait for her last stamp), JAI-02 follow the beat (taken). Different: no beat is followed and no strip is chosen; the one fact is that the third time is different, which the tabla announces by repeating twice first. Different from the door: the door rewards sam, this punishes being on a strip at sam.
- **Time:** 15-18 s (one tihai of three 3 s phrases with 2 s rests, plus approach).
- **Line:** "Teen baar, phir sam. Sam pe sab bajta hai." | "Thrice, then sam. On sam everything rings." (U wording; "sam" V).
- **Archetype:** B5 cadence then a liar (FREE): the pattern you learn is true twice.

#### 6.2 Dugun (Double time) — a ladder beat, law 6
- **Place:** the same stage; in a technical performance the tempo doubles or quadruples (V, Kathak page).
- **Rule:** tatkar's phrase comes again at double speed: the strips she taps ring twice as often, and the gaps between taps are too short to cross; the long gap is now between phrases.
- **Decision:** When the tempo doubles, cross between phrases, not between taps.
- **Obvious play:** cross between taps as tatkar taught: the next tap lands under you (`lko-tatkar`). **Fix:** wait for her to tire: the mehfil sweep comes (`lko-mehfil`). **Answer:** cross in the phrase gap (marked by her thaat pose, one still frame).
- **Closest:** own tatkar. A within-stage stale-rule beat, not a type. **Time:** 8-10 s. **Line:** "Dugun! Ab taal ke beech nahin, bol ke beech." | "Double time! Not between beats now, between phrases." (U). **Archetype:** law 6.

### Stage 09 Kabootarbazi: needs about 13 s

#### 9.1 Seeti (The whistle duel)
- **Place:** two lofts on two roofs, your flyer's and a rival's, the middle roof with a water tank between them. Flyers call birds with whistles and flags (U as practice; the bible already marks the hobby unverified).
- **Rule:** a whistle calls the flock down to the WHISTLER's roof whatever colour any flag shows; the rival and your flyer answer each other (a duel), each raising the whistle to his mouth 0.8 s before the sound (a big visible gesture).
- **Decision:** In a whistle duel, stand where neither flyer calls the birds: the roof of neither.
- **Obvious play:** cross on red, as the room taught: the rival whistles and the flock drops on his roof, which is yours at that moment (`lko-seeti`).
- **Obvious fix:** jump across to your flyer's roof when he whistles back: the flock swings to your flyer's roof (`lko-seeti-jawab`).
- **One-move answer:** the tank roof in the middle; wait out two whistles, then go on red as before.
- **Closest:** `lko09-jhande` (obey the nearer flag), `lko09-hawa`. Different: jhande asks which of two signals wins; seeti says a different KIND of signal overrides every flag (law 6: the flag rule goes stale), and the safe place is where neither signal points.
- **Time:** 12-15 s. **Line:** "Seeti baji, jhanda bhool jao." | "The whistle blew, forget the flag." (U).
- **Archetype:** law 6; B13 refuge shape (the tank roof is the recess; it is honest).

#### 9.2 Girahbaaz (The tumblers) — alternate
- **Place:** the same roofs. Tumbler pigeons roll over backwards in flight; tumbling birds existed in India before 1590 and "Indian Tumblers" is a breed (V, [Wikipedia: Tumbler pigeons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumbler_pigeons)).
- **Rule:** the flock now holds tumblers: now and then two or three birds drop toward the roof rolling, then recover; a drop of rolling birds is not a landing. A real landing is the whole flock gliding in a line, feet down.
- **Decision:** A tumble is not a landing; keep crossing on red through a tumble.
- **Obvious play:** birds coming down, get off the roof (the room's lesson): you jump back to the previous roof as the flag turns white and the real landing comes there (`lko-kabootar`). **Fix:** freeze mid-roof through everything: the flag turns white while you wait (`lko-kabootar` again: same cause, so this needs its own id, `lko-girah`). **Answer:** read the shape: rolling pair = go on; gliding line = off.

### Stage 10 Lakhnavi Tehzeeb (finale): needs about 44 s; each callback must break the rule it calls back

The builder plans photo, aadaab and wapas callbacks. These five are from 03-09 and do not use bells (the finale already has two bells clashes: julus and galiyara).

#### 10.1 Pehle aap, raasta (The parted strollers bow) — breaks 07's room
- **Rule called back:** pause and the strollers part. **Break:** the parted strollers are gentlemen; a gap made by politeness is a doorway, and 02's rule applies: they bow you through FIRST, and the stone past the gap is the loose one (`lko-pehle`).
- **Decision:** When strollers part for you, step back and let the nearer one go first; take the gap second.
- **Obvious play:** walk through the gap (07's answer): the loose stone. **Fix:** rush before they finish bowing: shoved (`lko-ganj`). **Answer:** pause, they part, bow back once (the aadaab length), he goes, you follow.
- **Time:** 8-10 s. **Line:** "Raasta diya? Pehle aap." | "They made way? After you." (V words).

#### 10.2 Batti aur malai (The lamplighter's light) — breaks 04 with 07
- **Rule called back:** 04's light comes from one side; read the shadow line once. **Break:** it is night; the only light is the lamplighter's lamp, and he walks. The shady side of every malai cup changes as he passes: the side that was safe is lit two steps later.
- **Decision:** Keep on the far side of the light source and move with it; never read the shade once.
- **Obvious play:** stand on the shady tile you read on arrival (04's answer): he passes, it is lit, it sinks (`lko-malai`). **Fix:** stay under his ladder where there is no light at all: the swing (`lko-seedhi`, 07's batti). **Answer:** walk the cups at his pace, one step behind the shadow's edge.
- **Time:** 10-12 s. **Line:** "Roshni chal rahi hai, chhaya bhi." | "The light walks, so does the shade." (U).

#### 10.3 Rangeen taaga (Coloured thread) — breaks 03
- **Rule called back:** blue print washes away; white stitch stays. **Break:** chikan is now also done in coloured thread (V, [Wikipedia: Chikan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chikan_(embroidery))): some blue lines are blue STITCHES, raised (drawn with stitch ticks), and stay after the wash; some white is unstitched ground, which was never solid.
- **Decision:** Read the texture (stitch ticks), not the colour.
- **Obvious play:** cross on white: unstitched white is not a floor (`lko-chhapa` at the whistle, the stage's wash). **Fix:** cross on the raised lines only after the whistle, to be sure: the second wash takes the jali (`lko-jali`). **Answer:** raised lines, any colour, before the first whistle.
- **Time:** 8 s. **Line:** "Rang nahin, taaga dekhiye." | "Not the colour, look at the thread." (U).

#### 10.4 Daana (Grain) — breaks 09
- **Rule called back:** cross only on red. **Break:** the flyer scatters grain on a roof (yellow dots, visible): the flock lands to feed whatever the flag shows.
- **Decision:** Grain beats the flag; cross a grain roof only once the flock has landed and is feeding (then it stays put for 3 s).
- **Obvious play:** cross on red: they land on the grain, on you (`lko-daana`). **Fix:** wait for white (they are "down anyway"): on white they land on the NEXT roof too, the one you are waiting on (`lko-kabootar`). **Answer:** let them land on the grain, then walk the grain roof's edge while they feed.
- **Closest in the taken list:** "a vendor feeding Dad" (Dad is never fed; birds are). **Time:** 8-10 s. **Line:** "Daana gira, jhanda bekaar." | "Grain's down, the flag means nothing." (U).

#### 10.5 Thanda tawa (The cold griddle) — breaks 05
- **Rule called back:** every heap squashes; the flat lane is the way; waiting on flat burns. **Break:** at night the tawa is off: cold heaps are set and do not squash; they are steps, and a flat one is safe to wait on. Only the one heap with sizzle lines (the cook's last) still squashes, and only it burns.
- **Decision:** Cold kebabs are stairs; climb them; squash only the one that sizzles.
- **Obvious play:** step on and wait to squash: nothing happens, and the skewer rack above the cold heaps comes down on its hoist (`lko-seekh`, 05's bhara rack). **Fix:** hop heap to heap at full height: the sizzling heap squashes under your landing and you drop under the tips (`lko-tawa-garam`). **Answer:** walk up the cold heaps as steps, squash the warm one, hop from it.
- **Time:** 8-10 s. **Line:** "Thanda kebab, seedhi ban gaya." | "Cold kebab, it's a stair now." (U).

#### 10.6 Roshni wala mehrab (Only lit arches lift) — breaks 08 with 04, only if 8.1 is built
- **Rule called back:** jump through an arch = a floor up. **Break:** in the dark an arch is only an arch; the lift happens only where a roshandan beam falls on it. **Decision:** jump the lit arches, walk the dark ones. **Obvious play:** jump every arch: the dark one does not lift, the well (`lko-kuan`). **Fix:** walk every arch: the lit one lifts a walker too? No: a walker stays, and the lit gallery's end is the hooks over him (`lko-bhool`). **Answer:** jump lit, walk dark. **Time:** 6-8 s. **Line:** "Roshni mein mehrab uthata hai." | "In the light the arch lifts." (U).
