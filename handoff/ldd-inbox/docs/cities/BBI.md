# BBI · Bhubaneswar, Odisha: city bible

Research for Level Devil Dad, chapter 18. Facts carry a link or say (unverified). Checked against
`docs/MECHANICS.md` and the Done tables. Taken and left alone: wind and the Kalbaishakhi gust (kit, KVT-04,
JAI-07), parachutes and gliding, swings (Raja's doli: JAI-04 and `swing`), chariots (RPR-08, `rath`), sand art
(`sandcastle`), Chilika dolphins (KVT-08), turtle nests (KVT-01: so no olive ridley arribada), lightning (RNC-04),
barrage gates (AMR-03: so no Hirakud gates), ropeways. Rasagola goes to neither BBI nor CCU (the rivalry is a
regional dig risk). Hooks kept for BBI in this batch: hockey, Pipili appliqué, palm-leaf etching, ikat that
shows its pattern only when woven, Konark's stone wheels, Gotipua poses, chhena poda.

## 1. Identity
- To Bhubaneswaris: the Temple City and a planned capital with wide roads; old stone towers next to IT parks,
  dahibara on every corner, and a state that hosts the world at hockey.
- Visitors come for: the cave hills of Udayagiri-Khandagiri, the Dhauli white stupa, Nandankanan zoo, then
  Konark, Pipili, Raghurajpur, Chilika and the Puri coast.
- Proud and laughing at: "Odia time" (unverified saying), pakhala on a hot day as the cure for everything, the
  aunty who insists you eat one more chhena poda.

## 3. Everyday life hooks
- **Transport.** City buses (Mo Bus, a real brand: draw generic blue buses, no name), autos, wide planned roads,
  long medians. Traffic on wide roads is generic India: avoid.
- **Street food.** Dahibara aloodum (lentil fritters soaked in thin curd, with spicy potato), chhena poda
  (cottage cheese with sugar, baked slow in a wood oven, caramelised crust) (unverified method), pakhala (rice
  soaked in water, with fried fish or badi; Pakhala Dibasa on 20 March, unverified), gupchup (Odia name for pani
  puri, unverified), podapitha (baked rice cake eaten at Raja, [wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Parba)).
  - Hidden-item candidates: **chhena poda** (first choice), **dahibara**, **podapitha**.
  - Traps: chhena poda crust: hard on top, soft inside: land and you sink to the soft middle only if you land
    from a jump; pakhala bowl: a floor of rice grains floating in water. (Gupchup is left to CCU's phuchka.)
- **Markets.** Ekamra Haat crafts market (unverified), Unit-1 market (unverified).
- **Weather.** Fierce April-May evening storms (Kalbaishakhi: taken as wind); humid monsoon; cyclones (avoid).
- **Animals.** Monkeys at the caves (taken), egrets, migratory birds at Chilika.
- **Crafts.** Pipili appliqué; pattachitra and palm-leaf etching (Raghurajpur); Sambalpuri ikat; Cuttack silver
  filigree; Dokra (taken by RNC).
- **Sports.** Hockey is huge (Sundargarh district is called the cradle of Indian hockey, unverified); kabaddi at
  Raja (taken).
- **Games.** Children on Raja swings (taken); bagudi (Odia tag game, unverified).
- **Festivals.** Raja (mid-June, three-four days; girls on swings, no barefoot walking, no cutting or grinding,
  farm work stops, podapitha; Gotipua dances at night, [wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Parba)): use only
  the "no work, everyone off the fields" rule and podapitha, never the reasons. Rath Yatra: religious, skip.
  Bali Jatra: boats, skip.
- **Gotipua.** Boy dancers in girls' costume holding acrobatic poses (bandha) (unverified details): poses as
  shapes.
- **Habits and sayings.** "Kemiti achha?"; "heba" (will do, unverified), "bhala" (good); offering more food.

## 5. Twelve stage concepts
**1. Namaskara Bhubaneswar** · tour · Caves, stadium, curved towers in the distance. Betrayal: the wide planned
road has a median of potted plants that a gardener waters from a hose; the water makes the kerb slippery for one
tile only where he aimed, and he follows the dad who waits. Second try: pass while he's turned to the pots.
Closest: TVM wet tiles? (TVM-01 soaked road breaks under a hop); different: aimed and moving, a slide not a break.

**2. Hockey (The drag-flick)** · standard · Kalinga-style turf. Expect: dodge a ball. Betrayal: the ball is
flicked low along the turf toward goal and you stand in the D; the goalkeeper's pads are the platforms and he
dives toward the ball, carrying you. Second try: stand on the pad on the far side from the flick. FIX: the dad
who hops early meets the second flick (rebound). Act: penalty corner: injector, stopper, flicker in a fixed
order; you cross between the stop and the flick. Door: the goal net: the door is inside it, and opens only after
a goal (you let the ball past). Closest: PNJ-03 football, `penalty`; different: ground-level ball, a keeper who
carries you, and you must let it score.

**3. Chandua (Pipili appliqué)** · standard · Parasol street. Expect: umbrellas as platforms (opens/closes like
blinkers?). Betrayal: the parasols open when a customer stands under one; the cloth cut-outs on them (elephant,
parrot, flower) are sewn on in different places and the open parasol's cut-out is where you can stand (the rest
is slippery silk). Second try: wait for a customer, land on the cut-out. Second betrayal: a shopkeeper peels a
cut-out off to sell it. Door: a lantern that turns as it's hung. Closest: blinkers (timed); different: a customer
opens it, and the safe part is a shape on its top.

**4. Talapatra (Palm-leaf etching)** · "how would I know" · A blank palm leaf as floor. Expect: nothing there.
Betrayal: the lines are cut but invisible; the platform is where the cut is, and you only see it after the
artist rubs the soot over it, which happens when you've passed. Second try: watch the soot pass once, memorise
(the cuts never change). FIX: the dad who follows the soot hand gets the stylus point as it cuts the next line.
Door: the leaf is bound into a book; the door is page two (you walk off the edge of page one). Closest: BPL-03
two worlds, `invisible`; different: the reveal is permanent and moves across the stage.

**5. Bandha (Ikat)** · long · Sambalpur loom. Expect: striped threads are floors. Betrayal: the dyed patches on
the warp are scattered; each beat of the loom shifts every thread by one step so the patches line up into a
pattern (and a bridge) only on certain beats. Second try: count beats until the motif is whole. Second betrayal:
the shuttle runs across the line at the bridge beat. Door: the finished motif is a door shape. Closest:
GNR-08 scrolling chart; different: alignment of many offset parts, no scroll.

**6. Chakka (The stone wheel)** · long · Konark-like plinth. Expect: the wheel will turn (JAI-05 rolling is taken,
`clockhand` too). Betrayal: the wheels never turn; the plinth slides past like a chariot when you stand on the
horses (stone blocks at the front), carrying the wheels into the spikes. Second try: stand on the wheel's
hub, which stays still as the plinth slides under it. Act: eight spokes as steps, numbered like hours; the ones
you stand on sink in order. Door: between two horses at the front. Closest: moving platforms, `clockhand`;
different: the "rider" pulls the stone, and the obviously-turning part is fixed.

**7. Chhena Poda (Baked overnight)** · standard · Wood oven. Expect: don't touch the hot thing. Betrayal: crust
is solid, but a jump landing breaks into the soft middle (walk on, never jump on). Second try: walk the crust.
FIX: the dad who walks carefully walks into the sal-leaf wrapping that slides off. Door: the oven door opens
when the poda is done (crust browns from the edges). Closest: TVM-01 soaked road (holds a walker, breaks a hop);
**too close**: keep only if the browning-edge timer and the leaf slide are the core; otherwise drop.

**8. Gotipua (The poses)** · marathon · Raja night dance (unverified fit; check). Expect: dancers in the way.
Betrayal: the troupe holds poses (bridge, tower, wheel) on the drum's stop; between stops they move. Each pose
is a different platform shape. Second try: wait for the bridge pose. Second betrayal: the pose you stand on
changes into the next when the drum restarts and throws you off. Door: the last pose is a gate. Closest: GNR-07
rings, RNC-05 masks; different: shapes chosen by the music stops.

**9. Raja (No one works)** · "how would I know" · Village in Raja week. Expect: the farm is a hazard course.
Betrayal: nobody works, nobody grinds or cuts for three days ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Parba)):
every machine in the stage is off, and the only moving things are the ones *you* start (stepping on a grinding
stone starts it). Second try: touch nothing. Door: the house with podapitha on the step. Respect note: never
show the reason; it's "the holiday when the fields rest".

**10. Tarakasi (Silver filigree)** · standard · Cuttack workshop. Expect: wire curls as fine platforms.
Betrayal: the silversmith solders a new curl on wherever you stand last; standing still builds a curl that
blocks the way. Second try: keep moving through, stop only at the gap. Door: a filigree box lid. Closest: RPR-06
cocoon from standing still; different: the build is in front of you and blocks, not protects.

**11. Chilika (The birds' carpet)** · long · Winter lagoon. Expect: water. Betrayal: a flock lands as a floor and
lifts when a boat motor sounds; you hear the motor before you see the boat. Second try: wait for silence. Second
betrayal: a second flock lands on a lower level, shifted. Door: the fishermen's hut on stilts. Caution: KVT
and DMN did lagoons; it's the birds here.

**12. Kalinga (Finale)** · finale · Callbacks only to BBI: the hockey keeper's pads carry you, the parasols with
cut-outs, the palm-leaf lines revealed by the soot, the ikat beats, the stone plinth that slides. New twist: the
soot hand reveals ikat motifs too.

Picks: 1 + 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12 (7 only if it clears the TVM-01 check).

## 6. Look and sound
- Palette: sky `#F6C177` (warm afternoon), hills `#8C6E4A` (laterite cave hills), tiles `#C1663B` (laterite
  red), accent `#E63946` (appliqué red), second accent `#2A9D8F` (appliqué green), plus parasol yellow `#F4D35E`.
- Skyline: curved stone spires (generic), two cave hills with stacked doorways, floodlights of a stadium, a
  white dome on a far hill (backdrop only), a giant half-buried stone wheel for Konark stages.
- Ground cap: laterite blocks with a carved band.
- Weather: hot sun, evening storm clouds (visual only), monsoon drizzle.
- Local script name: ଭୁବନେଶ୍ୱର (Odia).
- Music: 96 bpm, Odissi-flavoured, raga Kalyani/Bhupali family (unverified fit), mardala drum, manjira, flute.
- Postcard line: "Stone wheels that don't turn, and a city that never stops eating."
