## 1. Identity

- To the people who live there: the old summer capital strung along a ridge at about 2,200 m, a town of
  steps, slate roofs and deodars, where everyone walks the Mall in the evening and the apple harvest pays for
  the year.
- Visitors come for: the Ridge and the Mall, the Kalka-Shimla toy train, snow at Kufri, and the rest of
  Himachal: Manali and Solang, Kullu's shawls, Kasauli, Spiti.
- Proud and laughing: the public lift that saves your knees, a monkey that knows your bag better than you,
  and the first snow of the year that the whole town comes out to touch.

## 5. Twelve stage concepts

| # | Name (meaning) | Hook | Player expects | Betrayal | Second attempt rule | Second betrayal (act) | Door ending idea | Length | Closest used idea, and why this differs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Kiya Haal, Shimla? (How's it going, Shimla?) | Tour: Cart Road, the lift, the Mall, the Ridge, toy train on a far bridge | A gentle hill walk | The lift goes up, and its top door opens behind you (the far side, onto the Mall): the dad who walks forward hits the shut front door and rides back down | Turn round at the top | (tour) On the Ridge, the first snow lies on a lip that juts over the drop: the white edge is a cornice that breaks; the real edge is where the railing posts stand | — | standard | `pull`/`slidedoor` doors, crumbling floors; here a door on the other side and a snow lip read from the railing |
| 2 | Pakki Barf (Packed snow) | Fresh snow on the Ridge (unverified details) | Snow is ground | Fresh snow over a gap holds only where it has been stepped on before: every step packs the tile under it; a jump onto fresh snow sinks you | Pack your way: step, step back, step further | The packed path you made melts back to soft when the sun comes on it (the light band moves): the way back is not the way you made | `bel`: the door's step is snow; pack it three times (three landings) before it holds | marathon | DMN-07 painted path (fades behind), DMN-03 ice; here your own footsteps build the floor ahead, and it needs repeated steps |
| 3 | Chhat Ki Barf (Snow off the roof) | Slush sliding off slate roofs (unverified) | Eaves are shelter | When the sun reaches a roof, its snow slides off in one sheet onto the lane under the eave; the tell is a line of drips starting at that roof's edge | Pass under a roof only before its drips start or after its sheet has fallen | The sheet that fell is now a soft heap you sink in: step on the heap only on its packed edge | `parnala`: the door is behind the downpipe; it opens when the last roof's sheet has fallen | standard | Rockfall, TVM-04 coconuts, TVM-07 rocks; here the fall comes from a slope the sun reaches, with a drip tell that runs roof to roof |
| 4 | Gola (The snowball) | Children rolling snowballs at Kufri (unverified) | A rolling ball is something to jump | The snowball grows as it rolls down: small near the top (hop it), too big lower down; it stops only at the bottom where it is the base of a snowman (a step) | Go up to meet it early, or wait at the bottom for it to stop | The kids push a second, bigger ball down on the first and they stack: the snowman's head is the last step, but only once it's on | `topi`: the snowman needs a cap; stand on its head and the door opens, but it melts in the sun | standard | Rollers (kit), `amr03-logs`; here the hazard grows with distance and ends as a platform |
| 5 | Dhoop-Chhaon (Sun and shade) | Slope faces in sun and shade (unverified) | Snow is snow | Snow on the sun side is soft slush (you walk slowly, can't jump high); on the shade side it's hard and fast (you slide a tile further on landing) | Plan each jump by which side it's on | A cloud crosses the sun: the whole slope goes shade for a moment, and the slow side is fast | `dhoop`: the door is in the last patch of sun; it opens only while lit, and the lit patch moves | "how would I know" | DMN-03 melting ice, GNR-03 sunbeams; here the same snow has two physics by light, and light moves |
| 6 | Seedhiyan (The stair-lanes) | Lower Bazaar's steps and porters (unverified details) | Stairs are just stairs | A porter comes down with a load that fills the stair from wall to wall; stand in a doorway niche as he passes, or he carries you down with him | Find the niches before he comes (his bell rings two lanes up) | The second porter carries a ladder: it sticks out, and the niche you'd use is at ladder height (crouch-still at the bottom of the niche) | `aangan`: the door is at the top of the last stair; the porter's load is delivered there, and he opens the door for it | standard | Chasers, BOM-06 chawl staircase; here a slow body fills the route and the safe spots are architecture |
| 7 | Khaddi (The loom) | Kullu shawl handloom (unverified details) | Threads are scenery | The warp threads are two sheets that swap up and down at each treadle press; between them the shuttle shoots across | Stand on the lower sheet when it's lifting; jump off before the shuttle | The weaver changes the pattern: two presses on one side, then one: count her feet | `dupatta`: the door is the finished shawl's end; it rolls up on the beam as she weaves, and you step on only as it rolls | standard | Blinkers (kit), BPL-04 thread; here two linked floors swap with a visible pedal and a cross-shot |
| 8 | Lift Le Lo (Take the lift) | The public lift, ₹10 **[F]** | A lift goes where the arrow says | The attendant lets in twelve; you're the thirteenth and it won't move (the buzzer); step out and it leaves without you; the way up is the second lift whose doors open on the other side | Count the queue; take the empty lift | Halfway up there's a corridor between the two lifts; its far end floods with the crowd from the other lift: wait in the corridor's side | `ticket`: the door is the turnstile; it turns only for a ticket, and the ticket is in the hidden-item spot | "how would I know" | Rides that leave you; BLR-01 loading bridge; here the capacity rule and the second, opposite-door lift are the puzzle |
| 9 | Seb Ki Peti (The apple crates) | Crates stacked by the road (unverified) | A stack of crates is a staircase | Porters take crates from the bottom of the stack, so the whole stack drops a crate's height each time and the top crate (you) goes down | Ride the top down, get off when it's at the ledge height | A truck backs up and its tailboard is a step only while the porters are loading it | `bagicha`: the door is the orchard gate; it opens when the last crate is on the truck, so step off it | standard | Dropping floors (kit), DMN-04 hand-to-hand plank; here a stack shrinks from the bottom at a steady pace you can use |
| 10 | Gaddi (The shepherds' flock) | Seasonal flocks on the roads **[L]** | Sheep block the road | The flock is a moving floor: you can walk on their backs as they flow down the road, and they flow round anything in their way like water | Ride the flock; jump off at the gap they don't cross | The shepherd's whistle: on two notes the flock turns back uphill | `bhed`: the door is the fold's gate; the shepherd opens it when the last sheep is in, and that's you | marathon | `herd` (a market of candles), crowds; here a flowing flock carries you and parts round obstacles |
| 11 | Kath-Kuni (Wood and stone) | Himachal's walls of alternating timber and stone courses (unverified detail) [Kath-kuni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kath-kuni_architecture) **[L]** | A beam sticking out of a wall is a ledge | A timber beam holds you only where a stone course sits on top of it; the top beams with nothing above slide out of the wall when you land | Read the wall: stand on beams that have stone above | The mason takes stones off the top course to repair it: the beam that held a moment ago is now a top beam | `chaukhat`: the door is in the wall; its frame is timber, and it opens only once the mason sets the stone on its lintel | "how would I know" | BOM-06 wall kicks, crumbling floors; here a beam's strength follows the weight above it, visible in the wall |

## 6. Look and sound

  accent `#E2241F` (apple red), second accent `#F7F9FB` (snow).

  tower (no religious buildings), a toy train on a multi-tier stone arch bridge in the distance, deodars.
- Ground cap: snow on stone, with a slush line; wet slate steps.
- Weather: snowfall in soft flakes; sun patches moving with clouds.
- Script: Devanagari (Takri historically) (unverified) [Takri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takri_script) **[L]**.

  a nagara-like drum (unverified instruments), major pentatonic.
