# PAT · Patna, Bihar: city bible

Research for Level Devil Dad, chapter 16. Every fact has a link or says (unverified). Ideas checked against
`docs/MECHANICS.md` and the Done tables in `docs/CITIES.md` (15 cities, TVM to RNC).
Hooks given to Patna and kept away from the other six cities in this batch: the Ganga's bridges (Setu and the
pontoon), the granary, Mithila wall painting, litti in the embers, makhana. Rivers that move (sandbars) go to
DIS; ferries go to nobody.

## 1. Identity
- To Patnaites: Pataliputra, two and a half thousand years old, on a Ganga so wide you can't see Hajipur; home
  of litti chokha, coaching lanes on Ashok Rajpath and the loudest "ka ho" in India.
- Visitors come for: Golghar, Gandhi Maidan, Bihar Museum, then out to Nalanda, Rajgir, Vaishali, Sonepur mela.
- Proud and laughing at: "we've crossed the Setu in a jam longer than your trip", the dad who explains every exam
  and every film, sattu as the answer to every summer.

## 3. Everyday life hooks
- **Transport.** Shared tempo (the old "Vikram"-style three-wheeler, unverified name) seats six, carries twelve;
  the conductor hangs off the back and slaps the side to go. Trap: the slap means go, two slaps mean pull in;
  the tempo you ride tilts to whichever side has more riders. E-rickshaws everywhere now (unverified). Country
  boats on the Ganga: leave them, boats are taken.
- **Street food.** Litti is baked buried in the embers of a cow-dung-cake fire and dug out (unverified for
  Patna stalls; common knowledge). Chokha is mashed roasted brinjal, potato, tomato. Sattu sharbat in summer.
  Khaja: flaky layered sweet that sheds layers. Thekua: pressed in a carved wooden mould. Makhana: fox nuts dived
  from pond beds in Mithila, roasted till they pop (unverified details).
  - Hidden-item candidates: **litti** (first choice), **khaja**, **thekua**.
  - Traps: litti fire: embers are floor until someone pokes them, the "cooked" litti come up where the poker
    goes; khaja: a floor of flakes that loses one layer per landing (a counter, readable by thickness);
    thekua mould: stamp a pattern into dough and the pattern is the platform.
- **Markets.** Patna Market and Hathwa market crowds (unverified names); Sonepur stalls.
- **Weather.** Brutal May-June loo, then floods (Patna 2019, unverified details); dense winter fog over the
  Ganga (unverified). Fog over a bridge where you see only the next span is a hook nobody has.
- **Animals.** Nilgai in the fields, egrets, river dolphins at Vikramshila (dolphins are KVT's: skip).
- **Crafts.** Madhubani/Mithila painting; sikki grass (golden grass coiled into boxes and animals, unverified);
  Bhagalpur silk (skip: kosa is RPR's).
- **Sports and games.** Gilli-danda; pittu (stack of seven flat stones knocked down with a ball, rebuilt while
  the other side throws) (unverified local name). Trap: rebuild the stone stack as a stair while being chased.
- **Festivals.** Chhath: dawn and dusk offerings at the river. **Respect only: no gags, no traps on it.** At
  most a calm sunrise backdrop. Sama-Chakeva (Mithila, winter, clay birds): leave alone (unverified).
- **Habits and sayings.** "Ka ho"; "bujhaya?" (got it?); everyone's uncle explaining the exam syllabus; chai and
  newspaper at the corner; "Setu pe jam" as the city's unit of time.

## 5. Twelve stage concepts
**1. Prannam Patna (Hello, Patna)** · tour · Gandhi Maidan, Golghar, Setu in the backdrop. Expect: a free walk.
Betrayal: the tour bus-stop crowd surges to a tempo arriving behind you and carries you back two screens if you
stand in its path. Second try: step onto the kerb when the horn sounds. Act: none. Door: none. Closest: BOM-03
(train shoves you out); different because the crowd moves *along* the street toward a vehicle and you sidestep.

**2. Golghar (The round granary)** · standard · Two spiral stairs on one dome. Expect: climb either. Betrayal:
the up-stair is up-only: walk down it and the porters coming up push you off. Second try: down the other
spiral, where grain tipped in from the top rises inside the dome and pops out of the base door. FIX death: the
dad who rushes the down-stair meets the porter with the next sack at the turn. Act: fill the dome: each sack
you tip raises the grain floor inside; you need it high enough to reach a window. Door: `bhitar`: the door opens
inwards, into you; stand to its hinge side. Closest: RPR-04 (rice poured into hills); different: you don't pour,
the floor rises inside a closed room and the one-way stairs are the trap.

**3. Setu ka Jod (The bridge joint)** · long · Gandhi Setu's mid-span joints. Expect: a long flat bridge.
Betrayal: every joint bobs as a truck crosses: low when the truck is on your half, high when it's past. Jump
onto a low joint and the far half comes up into your shins. Second try: wait for the truck to pass the joint.
Second betrayal: fog; you see one span ahead, and one joint is missing its plate. Door: the toll booth that is no
longer a toll booth (toll revoked, [wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahatma_Gandhi_Setu)): the barrier stays
up and the booth window slides shut. Closest: CHN-08 lifting bridge; different: no lift, a see-saw coupling
driven by traffic weight.

**4. Pipa Pul (The pontoon bridge)** · long · Drum-floated deck. Expect: wobbly floor. Betrayal: each drum dips
under the heaviest thing on its section and lifts the next; a loaded handcart rolls with you and decides which
section sinks. Second try: let the cart roll ahead and walk on the high section behind it. FIX: the dad who
waits on the high section is there when the cart rolls back. Act: you push the cart to raise the drum ahead as a
step. Door: the last pontoon section is unhooked for the night and drifts off with the door. Closest: DMN-06
balance scale; different: a chain of floats, the load moves on its own, nothing to weigh.

**5. Litti ki Aanch (The litti embers)** · standard · Roadside litti fire. Expect: avoid the fire. Betrayal: the
embers are a safe floor; the danger is the poker, which flips embers up where it digs, and it digs where the
cooked litti are (marked by a crack in the ash). Second try: stand on uncracked ash. Act: carry a litti to the
chokha bowl before it cools (cooling = it gets heavy and you can't jump with it). Door: `chokha`: the door is
behind the mash pot that's being stirred. Closest: RNC-02 dhuska in the kadhai; different: floor is still,
the poker comes from above and targets the food marker, not you.

**6. Madhubani (The double line)** · standard · Mithila wall painting. Expect: painted figures are scenery.
Betrayal: every figure is drawn in a double outline; the inner line is solid, the band between lines is air.
Fish, peacocks and suns fill the wall, no blank space. Second try: land on the inner line. Second betrayal: the
painter fills a gap while you watch (no empty space allowed): the hatch fills in and becomes a wall where you
were going to stand. Door: the painted door with a double frame: walk through the band. Closest: BPL-03
painted/real worlds, DMN-07 painted path, `paintwall`; different: geometry of the outline, not a switch or a
trail.

**7. Tempo (The shared tempo)** · marathon · Long road on a shared tempo. Expect: ride to the end. Betrayal: the
tempo leans toward the side with more riders; riders board on the kerb side at stops. Second try: stand on the
road side at stops to balance it. Second betrayal: conductor's slaps: one slap go, two slaps pull in hard (you
fly forward). Not TVM-02's bells: the slaps are on the side panel and you see the hand; *and* the rule is lean,
not brake. FIX: the dad who balances on the road side gets the mirror of a passing truck. Door: the tempo stand,
where the door is the gap between two parked tempos that closes when a third backs in. Closest: TVM-02 bus bells, HYD autos; different: the ride's tilt is a
function of where riders stand.

**8. Sattu (The summer drink)** · "how would I know" · Loo wind afternoon. Expect: a heat stage (CHN-05 mirage
is taken, so no mirages). Betrayal: drinking sattu from a stall makes Dad heavy and cool: he no longer floats
over the hot-air vents that a light dad rides, but he can walk the hot tar that kills a thirsty dad. You can't
know this until you drink. Second try: drink at the second stall, not the first. Door: the door is behind the
stall's water pot, which you must leave alone. Closest: AMR-01 being fed (shorter jump); different: two states
change two rules (vents vs tar), and you choose when.

**9. Khaja (The layered sweet)** · standard · Silao's flaky khaja (unverified place). Expect: a crumbling
floor. Betrayal: each landing peels one layer; thickness shows layers left. A short hop on the same tile peels
two (the hop counts take-off and landing). Second try: walk across thick tiles, never hop on one. Act: stack of
khaja you climb; jumps peel layers above you that fall on you. Door: the door is on the last single layer.
Closest: crumbling floors (kit); different: a visible counter and the take-off counts.

**10. Sonepur Mela (The great fair)** · marathon · Fair crowd and stalls. Expect: dodge animals. Betrayal: the
crowd parts for a horse rider and closes behind him; you can only move in the gap he makes. Second try: follow a
horse, not the open lane. Second betrayal: the giant wheel's gondolas (not a ride: seats you stand on as they
pass) hold one rider; if a fairgoer is in it, it swings you off. Door: the railgram stall (the fair's mail
counter, unverified) stamps your ticket and the stamp is the key. Closest: GNR-07 garba rings, JAI-06 horse;
different: you ride nothing, you move in a moving gap.

**11. Nalanda (The brick university)** · "how would I know" · Rows of identical brick cells. Expect: enter a
cell, exit the same one. Betrayal: every cell is a passage to the cell with the same number of bricks over
its door in the next courtyard. You can count them, nobody tells you. Second try: count bricks. Door: the
library mound: walk into the one cell with no bricks counted (zero). Closest: `invisible`, `fakewin`,
LKO's maze (given to LKO only as a different thing); different: a counting rule, no pick-the-real-door.

**12. Setu se Golghar (Finale)** · finale · Callbacks to Patna only: the Setu joints carry you to the pontoon,
the cart decides the sinking drum, the khaja floor counts your hops, the Golghar spiral is one-way and the door
opens inwards. New twist: fog over the Setu hides which joint the truck is on: you read the truck's lights.

Picks for a 10-stage city: 1 (tour) + 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10, 12.

## 6. Look and sound
- Palette: sky `#F2D7A6` (dusty summer haze), hills/far bank `#B7B08C` (Ganga sandbank), tiles `#9C5A3C` (old
  brick), accent `#D7262E` (Madhubani red), second accent `#F2B705` (sikki gold).
- Skyline: Golghar dome, long girder bridge low on the horizon, a ghat with steps, a water tank, coaching
  hoardings. Ground cap: sun-baked brick paving, dust at the edges.
- Weather: loo shimmer by day, winter fog, monsoon rain sheets.
- Local script name: पटना (Devanagari).
- Music: 100 bpm, Bihari folk feel in a major pentatonic close to raga Bilawal (unverified); dholak and
  jhaal (small cymbals) on a kaharwa 8-beat; a harmonium drone.
- Postcard line: "Two and a half thousand years old, and still stuck on the Setu."

## 7. Avoid (hard no)
Caste; politics; "Bihari" stereotypes and jokes about migrants; liquor (dry state); Chhath gags of any kind;
Bodh Gaya, Patna Sahib and all temples or shrines as traps; coaching-institute names and real teachers; floods
as disaster comedy; elephant trade at Sonepur; real exam names.
